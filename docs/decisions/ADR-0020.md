## Context

A florist business routinely has to send branch inventory **back to the
supplier** — flowers that arrived already wilted or expired, damaged or
short-delivered goods, items rejected on inspection, or stock quarantined and
then returned under a commercial arrangement. This is a real, recurring
operational and financial event, and today the frozen design names the pieces
(`purchase_return`, `supplier_credit_note`, the `SUPPLIER_RETURN`
inventory-movement category, `supplier_balance` as the AP subledger) without
locking how they behave.

**What this ADR resolves** — the operational shape a real accounts-payable
process needs:

- a **supplier return is not a sale** and must never create revenue — it is an
  inventory-out movement plus, when the supplier agrees, a reduction of what the
  business owes that supplier;
- **dispatching stock does not by itself create supplier financial credit** —
  the supplier's confirmation does;
- **supplier credit is its own balance**, structurally separate from a supplier
  **advance** (money the business paid the supplier ahead of a bill);
- the value a return is _expected_ to be worth and the value the supplier
  _confirms_ are frequently different, and that difference must never be
  silently absorbed;
- one supplier credit note may be applied across several future purchase
  invoices, and several credit notes across one invoice, with an **unapplied
  balance carried forward** — and the original credit note amount is never
  mutated to represent that;
- the **physical** return originates at a branch; the **financial** supplier
  credit defaults to company + supplier scope, with full branch-source
  traceability;
- later accounting integration must keep four things conceptually distinct that a
  naive implementation would blur into one: the inventory return, the AP
  reduction, the unresolved expected-vs-confirmed difference, and any inventory
  loss.

**What this ADR deliberately does not decide** — the inventory costing method,
the tax calculation / input-tax adjustment rules, the specific fiscal-document
system for any jurisdiction, and the exact journal entries. Those belong to the
future Inventory / Accounting / fiscal work and to the jurisdiction-configurable
localization architecture.

This mirrors, on the supplier/AP side, what [ADR-0019](ADR-0019.md) locked on the
customer/AR side (credit ≠ payment method; append-only subledger; allocation
records, not mutable balances; a resolution validated against actual/confirmed
value, not a nominal total; a reconciliation invariant; reuse of the existing GL
posting discipline, no new mechanism).

## Decision

### 1. A supplier return is an inventory-out event, never a sale

A **purchase return** (also "supplier return") dispatches branch inventory back
to a supplier. It:

- generates a **`SUPPLIER_RETURN`** `inventory_movement` (branch-scoped, signed
  negative, append-only — the existing single inventory engine, §16·20·21, no
  parallel mechanism), carrying `lot_id?`, a unit-value field, `ref_kind =
PURCHASE_RETURN`, `ref_id`, an `idempotency_key`, and the acting user;
- **recognizes no revenue, no sale, no customer-side effect, and no fake
  revenue** — it is the reversal of an inbound stock position, never a disposal
  at a selling price;
- reduces the **inventory asset** and creates, where the supplier accepts the
  return, a **claim on the supplier** (supplier credit) — never a cash inflow
  unless and until the supplier actually refunds cash (§10).

Physical disposition (which lot, which branch, expired vs damaged vs rejected)
is decided from the item's actual physical state — never inferred from the
financial outcome, mirroring ADR-0019 §29/§30.

> **Deferred — inventory valuation / cost-basis.** Inventory valuation /
> cost-basis treatment for supplier returns will be determined by the future
> inventory / accounting policy and must remain consistent with the inventory
> costing method selected for the company. This ADR does **not** choose weighted
> average, FIFO, specific identification, or any other costing method — that
> decision belongs to the future Inventory / Accounting phase.

### 2. Supplier credit is its own balance — never a supplier advance, never income, payment or discount

**Supplier credit** = value owed **by the supplier to the business**. It arises
from reasons such as a purchase return, an expired- or damaged-stock return, an
overbilling correction, a shortage adjustment, a commercial credit note, or
another approved supplier adjustment. The reason set is **configuration, not a
fixed code enum** (`ARCHITECTURE.md` "extensible concepts are strings + reference
tables" convention / ADR-0018 §6); the final reference-table contents are a
Phase 5 implementation detail.

Supplier credit is **structurally distinct** from, and never conflated in
schema, code or reporting with:

| Not the same as                                                               | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Supplier advance** (money the business _paid_ the supplier ahead of a bill) | an advance is the business's asset because it _paid out_; a credit is the business's claim because goods _went back_ or the supplier _over-billed_. Opposite origin. Kept in separate subledger balances, never netted destructively — same discipline as ADR-0019 §28 (customer receivable vs. customer advance). |
| **Income / other income**                                                     | no revenue event occurs — §1. No fake revenue.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **A payment**                                                                 | no cash moves (§10).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **A purchase / sale discount**                                                | a discount changes a price at transaction time; a supplier credit is a post-event claim against an already-posted payable or a returned asset.                                                                                                                                                                     |

### 3. Expected vs confirmed credit — the difference is always explicit

A supplier return is dispatched before the supplier has agreed a value.
**Dispatching stock does not automatically create supplier financial credit** —
it opens an _expected_ claim. The supplier's confirmation (a credit note or
equivalent acknowledgement) is what **creates / authorizes** the supplier credit
note and the supplier-credit balance.

The model records **three** figures, never one:

- **Expected credit** — the business's own valuation of the returned goods at
  dispatch. The valuation basis (a cost-derived amount, the original
  purchase-line price, or another basis) follows the future inventory /
  accounting policy and the company's selected costing method — **not fixed
  here**.
- **Confirmed credit** — the value the supplier actually acknowledges, captured
  from the supplier's credit note / confirmation.
- **Difference** — `expected − confirmed`, a first-class recorded amount.

Worked example (owner's): Expected AED 500, Supplier confirms AED 420 →
Difference AED 80, recorded explicitly.

The difference **remains open and visible** on every relevant view until it is
resolved through an approved process. Conceptual resolution outcomes (the final
set is a Phase 5 implementation detail): a pending supplier claim, a dispute, an
authorized adjustment, an inventory loss / wastage where applicable, or another
approved resolution. It is **never silently discarded** and never folded into the
confirmed number; its resolution is an explicit, permissioned, audited action
(reason, resolved-by, approved-by where policy requires a distinct approver,
timestamp, tenant/company/branch context) — the same audit shape ADR-0019 §5/§24
uses for a settlement discount or a cancellation-charge override.

### 4. The lifecycle

```
Expired / Damaged / Quarantine / Rejected branch stock
  → Purchase Return created (draft)            — lines, reason, expected value
  → Dispatch                                   — SUPPLIER_RETURN inventory_movement(s); stock leaves; NO financial credit yet
  → Supplier Confirmation                      — confirmed value recorded; expected-vs-confirmed difference opened
  → Supplier Credit Note                       — the supplier's document; the confirmed credit amount; creates/authorizes the credit
  → Supplier Credit Ledger entry               — an append-only balance-increasing entry
  → Apply Credit to a future Purchase Invoice  — SupplierCreditAllocation record(s), never a mutation
  → Net Supplier Payable reduced               — the AP balance moves by the allocated amount
```

Every stage is a state transition or an append-only entry — **never a
destructive edit** of a prior stage once a financial effect exists (§F / the GL
correction discipline / ADR-0019 §12/§17).

### 5. Future domain concepts (names indicative — final schema is the implementation-phase concern)

| Concept                        | Role                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **`PurchaseReturn`**           | header: supplier, source branch, status (indicative: draft → dispatched → confirmed → credit-noted → partially-applied / applied · cancelled), reason, created-by, dispatch reference, links to its inventory movements and its credit note            |
| **`PurchaseReturnLine`**       | per returned item: item, lot/batch, expiry, **normalized quantity** (base UOM — Task 3.6's `variant.baseUomCode` is the anchor), original purchase-line reference where known, **expected value**, **confirmed value**                                 |
| **`SupplierCreditNote`**       | the supplier's credit document: supplier, company, currency, **confirmed credit amount (immutable once issued)**, reason, source `PurchaseReturn` (nullable — a commercial or overbilling credit note has no return), issue date, attached document(s) |
| **`SupplierCreditLedger`**     | the append-only supplier-credit subledger — one balance per `(company, supplier)`; a projection, never a hand-edited column (§7)                                                                                                                       |
| **`SupplierCreditAllocation`** | an append-only record applying some or all of one `SupplierCreditNote` to one purchase invoice / supplier bill; the mechanism by which credit reduces AP                                                                                               |

### 6. Applying credit to future purchase invoices — allocations, never mutation

Supplier credit reduces what the business owes by **allocation records**, exactly
as ADR-0019 §8 handles a customer payment applied to an invoice:

- **partial application** — part of a credit note against one invoice;
- **full application** — a credit note fully consumed by one invoice;
- **many credit notes → one invoice** — several `SupplierCreditAllocation` rows
  targeting the same bill;
- **one credit note → many invoices** — several allocation rows from the same
  credit note;
- **unapplied balance carried forward** — a credit note not yet fully allocated
  keeps a positive remaining balance, available for a later invoice.

Worked example (owner's): Purchase invoice AED 1,000; available supplier credit
AED 200; credit applied AED 200; **net payable AED 800**.

**The original `SupplierCreditNote` amount is never mutated** to represent usage.
Its remaining balance is `issued amount − Σ SupplierCreditAllocation` — derived,
never a stored editable field. A wrong allocation is corrected by a reversal +
a new allocation, never an edit (§F / ADR-0019 §12).

### 7. Supplier-credit ledger — append-only, reversible; reconciliation invariant

The supplier-credit subledger is **append-only and reversible**: a correction is
a reversal entry plus a new entry, never an edit or an erasure (the GL correction
discipline, §F / ADR-0019 §12/§17).

For any supplier credit note, and summed per `(company, supplier)`:

```
supplier_credit_remaining
  = Σ credit notes created / authorized
  − Σ credit applied to purchase invoices
  − Σ credit refunded in cash by the supplier
  + Σ reversals / authorized adjustments (signed)
```

Always **provable from the append-only supplier-credit subledger event stream**,
never trusted from a stored balance — the same discipline as
`branch_inventory_balance` ↔ `inventory_movement`, the GL ↔ its journal entries,
and ADR-0019 §15's receivables invariant. A reconciliation job recomputes it and
alerts on drift, mirroring the existing Inventory-control-account and AR
reconciliations (§F.10).

**Conceptual** entry kinds — `CREDIT_NOTE_CREATED · CREDIT_APPLIED ·
CREDIT_REVERSAL · CREDIT_ADJUSTMENT`, plus a distinct kind for the
expected-vs-confirmed difference and its resolution. **The final schema enum set
is a Phase 5 implementation detail** unless an approved accounting ADR freezes it
earlier.

### 8. Branch / company scope — physical at the branch, financial at the company

- The **physical** return (`PurchaseReturn`, `PurchaseReturnLine`, the
  `SUPPLIER_RETURN` movements) **originates from a branch** and is branch-scoped
  for inventory, isolation and audit — the same rule as every other stock
  movement (CLAUDE.md rule 8).
- The **financial** supplier credit **defaults to `(company, supplier)` scope** —
  companies are legal entities with their own GL (ZF-3) and may be in different
  countries / currencies (ADR-0018 / ADR-0019 D2-2), so a supplier balance and
  its credit are company-scoped, not tenant-wide and not branch-wide.
- Every supplier-credit and difference record **retains exact source-branch and
  return-document traceability**: source branch, `PurchaseReturn` reference,
  batch/lot, expiry, item, **normalized quantity** (base UOM), expected value,
  confirmed value, acting user, timestamp, and audit history.

**Locked: a company + supplier scoped supplier-credit balance, with exact
branch-source and return-document traceability on every entry.**

### 9. Transactional integrity — reuses the existing pattern, no new mechanism

The stock-out movement(s), the inventory-asset effect, the supplier-credit
subledger entry, the AP effect on allocation, the expected-vs-confirmed
difference record, and the audit records commit **together in one DB
transaction**, matching the already-approved stock+GL+cash+order-state pattern
(CLAUDE.md rule 29) and inventory↔GL atomicity (rule 25 / §F.10). External side
effects (a supplier notification, a document upload) go through the existing
transactional outbox (rule 28). **No new transactional mechanism.**

### 10. Accounting, tax and fiscal documents — conceptual only; specifics deferred

**Accounting — conceptual distinctions, not journal entries.** Supplier credit
is **not** income, **not** a payment, **not** a discount, **not** a supplier
advance, and **no fake revenue** is ever recognized. **No cash movement occurs**
unless the supplier actually refunds cash. This ADR does **not** freeze
debit/credit journal entries. Later accounting integration must, at minimum, keep
these **four** things distinct — never blurred into one amount:

1. **the inventory return / value movement** — stock leaving to the supplier
   (distinct from a wastage write-off: the goods are going back, not being
   destroyed);
2. **the supplier credit / accounts-payable reduction** — the confirmed credit
   and its allocation against future purchase invoices (distinct from a supplier
   cash payment — no cash moves);
3. **the unresolved supplier claim / expected-vs-confirmed difference** — held
   distinctly until resolved (distinct from the confirmed credit the supplier has
   agreed);
4. **inventory loss / wastage where applicable** — a difference resolved as a
   loss, or stock condemned rather than returned (a real expense, distinct from
   a claim).

Exact account keys, journal entries and posting templates are a future
accounting-phase concern; where an existing approved accounting ADR already
defines a relevant entry, that entry governs — this ADR adds none.

**Tax — deferred to jurisdiction / fiscal rules.** Tax treatment of supplier
credit notes / purchase returns must follow the applicable jurisdiction, the
original purchase tax treatment, the supplier tax document, and the applicable
fiscal posting rules. This ADR does **not** state that a supplier return
necessarily performs an input-tax reversal, and it hardcodes **no** UAE / KSA /
Qatar / Kuwait / Bahrain / Oman behaviour (CLAUDE.md §9, rules 32–33; §45
localization is jurisdiction-configurable).

**Statutory / e-invoicing documents — generic gate, not a specific system.**
Where a jurisdiction requires statutory credit-note / e-invoicing documents for a
purchase return or a supplier credit note, the supplier-return / supplier-credit
workflow must integrate with the applicable fiscal-document provider / gate
defined by the localization and e-invoicing architecture (§45; the
`EInvoicingProvider` port family). A specific national system (e.g. KSA ZATCA /
Fatoora) may be **one later implementation** of that generic gate — it is
**not** a generic GCC core dependency of this ADR.

### 11. Reporting

Supplier-credit balances, unallocated credit, open expected-vs-confirmed
differences and their ageing, and supplier-return volume / value feed the
existing `rpt_supplier_balance` family and the AP section of Owner financial
management (§F.11 / §F.12). Supplier credit is reported as a **reduction of what
is owed**, never as income or as cash received.

### 12. Roadmap placement

Implementation lands with the future **Inventory + Purchasing + Accounts
Payable** work:

- **Phase 5** (raw-material inventory, movement ledger, procurement basic) —
  already scopes `supplier`, `supplier_balance` (AP), `purchase` /
  `goods_receipt`, `supplier_bill`, and the `SUPPLIER_RETURN` movement category.
  The `PurchaseReturn` / `PurchaseReturnLine` physical flow and its
  inventory-movement posting belong here, **valued per the costing method that
  phase selects** (§1).
- **The supplier-credit ledger, the expected-vs-confirmed difference workflow,
  `SupplierCreditNote` / `SupplierCreditLedger` / `SupplierCreditAllocation`, and
  the AP-reduction allocation model** land with the accounts-payable depth of
  Phase 5's procurement work. Any statutory-document depth follows the
  localization / e-invoicing architecture's own gating (§10), unchanged — the
  same operational-now / statutory-later split ADR-0019 recorded for the AR side.

**No change to any phase's dependency order or exit criteria.** A credit-capable
purchasing system cannot ship without its own return / credit-note / AP-reduction
workflow, so this is concrete operational detail on already-planned modules, not
new scope.

## Consequences

- The supplier / AP side now has the same locked **operational** model the
  customer / AR side got in ADR-0019: append-only reversible subledger, allocation
  records not mutable balances, value validated against actual / confirmed amounts
  not nominal totals, a reconciliation invariant, and reuse of the existing GL +
  outbox discipline.
- Phase 5 planning inherits a concrete `PurchaseReturn` → `SupplierCreditNote` →
  `SupplierCreditAllocation` model to design against, with the four **conceptual**
  accounting distinctions (§10) called out.
- **Deferred, on purpose:** the inventory costing method, the tax
  calculation / input-tax rules, the jurisdiction fiscal-document system, and the
  exact journal entries — all left to the future Inventory / Accounting / fiscal
  work and the jurisdiction-configurable localization architecture.
- No Phase 3a artifact changes. Task 3.6 stays UOM / pack conversion only. No
  runtime code, schema, or migration.
- The expected-vs-confirmed difference is a permanent first-class concept — no
  implementation may collapse it into the confirmed amount.
