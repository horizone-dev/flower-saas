## Context

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §42·43 and §F already establish real financial foundations this
ADR builds on, not replaces: money as integer minor units (`packages/money`, no
floats), a double-entry GL that is a projection never an input (ZF-1), synchronous
inline posting for core events (ZF-2), one GL per company with branch/POS/shift as
journal-line dimensions (ZF-3), contra-revenue for discounts (ZF-7), perpetual
weighted-average COGS (ZF-8), and — specifically relevant here — **posting
templates already exist** for a credit sale (`Dr AR / Cr Revenue+VAT`), a customer
paying down credit (`Dr Cash / Cr AR`), an advance received (`Dr Cash / Cr Customer
Advances`), and an advance applied (`Dr Customer Advances / Cr Revenue+VAT`). The
`receivables` module (formerly `ledger`) is already scoped in Phase 3 for "customer
AR subledger, credit limits, customer advances, gift cards, statements,"
reconciling to AR/Advances control accounts (§module table); `DOMAIN-MODEL.md`
already names `CustomerReceivable` (a balance backed by an `ar_transaction`
subledger) and `CustomerAdvance` (a liability + `advance_transaction` subledger).

**What is genuinely missing, and what this ADR resolves:** the frozen design has
the _accounting_ half of receivables (the GL postings for credit-sale/repayment/
advance) but not the _operational_ half a real florist-credit business needs day to
day — a formal invoice payment-status state machine (PAID vs SETTLED did not
exist), a settlement workflow (FIFO default, manual-allocation toggle,
settlement-discount toggle), a customer ledger/subledger entry-type taxonomy, and a
settlement-discount-vs-sale-discount distinction. A first draft of this ADR
resolved all of that; this revision additionally closes three precision gaps a
second owner review round identified in that first draft:

- The first draft's `PAYMENT`/`PAYMENT_ALLOCATION` entry kinds, read literally,
  could be implemented so that receivables are reduced once when a `PAYMENT` is
  recorded and again when it is `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`-ed to an invoice — genuine
  double-counting risk if not made explicit. §8 below closes this.
- The first draft's `SETTLEMENT` entry kind, read literally, could be implemented
  as its own balance-affecting ledger amount **on top of** its component entries
  (payment allocation, discount, advance application) — also a double-counting
  risk. §9 below makes `SETTLEMENT` a grouping/workflow header only.
- "Oldest eligible outstanding invoice first" (FIFO) was not given a deterministic
  ordering rule, and the interaction between AUTO allocation and a manually-entered
  settlement discount was left unspecified. §3 and §6 below close these.

This ADR locks the operational + accounting model for all of the above, additive to
§42·43/§F, without touching their existing text.

## Decision

### 1. Credit / receivables

Credit is **not a payment method** — it is the absence of settlement. A credit sale
still fully recognizes revenue + VAT at the point of sale (per the existing `Dr AR /
Cr Revenue+VAT` posting template); what it creates is an **Accounts Receivable
obligation**, not a deferred sale. Per customer:

- `credit_enabled` (boolean, default off — a customer must be explicitly extended
  credit)
- `credit_limit` (money, nullable = no numeric ceiling beyond `credit_enabled`)
- `current_outstanding` (derived — see §10/§15, never a hand-edited column)
- `available_credit` = `credit_limit − current_outstanding` (derived)
- `advance_balance` (derived from the `advance_transaction` subledger — money the
  customer has paid in that has not yet been applied to an invoice; see §11 for how
  an unallocated payment becomes an advance)

A sale that would push `current_outstanding` past `credit_limit` is blocked unless
an authorized override is used — **credit-limit override requires its own
permission, is step-up/approval-gated per tenant policy, and is fully audited**,
the same pattern already governing the custom-bouquet margin-below-threshold
approval (Z-9) and money-affecting step-up actions generally (CLAUDE.md rule 13).

### 2. Invoice payment status — system-derived, never hand-edited

A new, explicit state machine, orthogonal to both the `order` fulfilment state
machine and the `payment` state machine (all three exist independently on the same
order/invoice; none subsumes another):

`UNPAID → PARTIAL → {PAID | SETTLED} · PARTIALLY_REFUNDED → REFUNDED ·
CANCELLED/VOID`

**Computed only** from the authoritative subledger events (§10/§15) — no API,
role, or permission ever writes this field directly. It is a read model over the
events, recomputed (or incrementally updated inside the same transaction that
records the event), never a free-standing editable status. Precisely:

- **UNPAID** — no obligation has been satisfied; no valid allocation or adjustment
  has yet reduced this invoice's outstanding from its original amount.
- **PARTIAL** — outstanding remains **> 0** after all valid allocations/adjustments
  applied so far (some money and/or eligible advance has been applied, but not
  enough to reach zero).
- **PAID** — outstanding **= 0**, and the obligation was satisfied **entirely by
  actual monetary value** — payment allocation(s) and/or an eligible
  previously-received advance applied (§11) — with **no** settlement discount,
  write-down, or other non-payment adjustment involved anywhere in that invoice's
  satisfying entries.
- **SETTLED** — outstanding **= 0**, and **at least part** of the obligation was
  extinguished by a settlement discount / write-down / other approved non-payment
  settlement adjustment (even if the remainder was genuinely paid in cash). An
  invoice satisfied by ANY non-payment adjustment is `SETTLED`, never `PAID` — the
  two are never interchangeable, and a `SETTLED` invoice's actual-paid figure must
  never be reported as though it equalled the invoice total (the owner's
  INV-1000/paid-950/discount-50/outstanding-0 example is the canonical worked
  case: status is `SETTLED`, not "Paid 1000"). Settlement discount/write-off is
  **never** reported as money paid, in any view or export.
- `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` / `REFUNDED` / `CANCELLED`/`VOID` remain governed by their
  own existing lifecycle rules (the existing `payment`/`order` state machines) —
  this ADR does not redefine them, only positions them as terminal/exceptional
  states of the same `invoice_payment_status` field.
- **Every invoice view (detail screen, list, export, report) exposes, as separate
  figures, never collapsed into one number:** invoice total, actual paid,
  settlement discount / approved adjustment, outstanding, status. This is a binding
  UI/reporting contract, not a suggestion (same weight as §7's settlement-screen
  contract).

### 3. Customer settlement — one payment across one or more open invoices, deterministic AUTO FIFO

A **settlement** is the act of applying one received payment across a customer's
open (unpaid/partially-paid) invoices (see §9 for why `SETTLEMENT` itself never
carries a balance-affecting amount). Default allocation is **AUTO FIFO** — oldest
eligible outstanding invoice first, exactly per the owner's worked example
(INV-101/102/103 due 300/200/500, payment 650 → 300 / 200 / 150).

**"Oldest" is defined deterministically, never by database row-return order:**
ordering is by the invoice's **authoritative posting/issue ordering** — the same
ordering already authoritative for the sale itself (the order's gapless numbering
/ posting sequence defined by the `orders`/`accounting` modules, e.g. issue
timestamp or posting sequence number), with a **stable tie-breaker** (invoice
id/sequence) for any two invoices that would otherwise compare equal. This
ordering is computed server-side from the same authoritative source the sale
numbering already uses — never re-derived ad hoc by a settlement query with no
defined `ORDER BY` tie-breaker.

"Eligible" excludes any invoice not in an allocatable state — see §4's exclusion
list (cancelled/void/refunded invoices are never allocation targets).

### 4. Manual payment allocation — an explicit, default-off toggle

The settlement screen carries a **"Manual Payment Allocation" toggle, default
OFF**. OFF → automatic FIFO (§3). ON → the authorized user allocates the received
payment invoice-by-invoice. Manual mode is **server-validated**, not merely
client-enforced (the same "a confirm action always re-validates server-side" rule
already governing realtime and every other client-supplied action, ADR-0017 §8 /
CLAUDE.md rule 30): total allocated ≤ payment received; no single invoice allocated
above its own eligible outstanding; no negative remaining amount; closed/cancelled/
refunded invoices are never eligible allocation targets; any unallocated remainder
is clearly surfaced per §11 (never silently allocated).

### 5. Settlement discount — an explicit, default-off toggle, invoice-total-preserving

**Settlement Discount, default OFF.** OFF → no settlement-discount entry is
available at all (not merely hidden — the capability does not exist for that
settlement). ON → a settlement discount may be entered manually, invoice-by-invoice,
against the settlement being processed. It requires, on every entry: reason
(free-text or a reason-code, tenant-configurable), applied-by (the authenticated
user — `acting_user_id`, never overwritten, matching CLAUDE.md rule 12), an
approval decision per tenant policy/threshold (below-threshold may auto-approve per
policy; above-threshold requires an approver + step-up, mirroring Z-9's margin
approval), approved-by when an approval was required, tenant/company/branch/POS
context where applicable, a timestamp, and a permanent audit history entry (§12 —
never editable after the fact). **A settlement discount never rewrites the original
invoice total** — it is a separate ledger entry (§10) that reduces `outstanding`,
never a mutation of the invoice's `total` field.

### 6. AUTO allocation + settlement discount interaction — server-authoritative recalculation

When **Payment Allocation = AUTO** and **Settlement Discount = ON**, the user
manually specifies an invoice-wise settlement discount while payment allocation
itself stays automatic. The server — **never the client** — deterministically
recalculates the automatic payment allocation against each invoice's **eligible
remaining outstanding** (original outstanding minus that invoice's entered
discount, if any) such that:

- payment allocation remains FIFO (§3's deterministic ordering, unchanged);
- the selected discount stays attached to the invoice it was entered against;
- for any invoice, `payment allocated + discount applied` never exceeds that
  invoice's outstanding — a discount reduces how much payment that invoice still
  needs, it never lets the invoice absorb more value than it owes;
- reducing an invoice's outstanding via a discount **frees payment capacity**,
  which the server re-flows forward, in FIFO order, to the next eligible
  outstanding invoice (a discount on an earlier invoice can cause payment that
  would have gone to it to spill forward to a later invoice instead — recalculated
  automatically, not left for the user to re-enter);
- the **client renders, but does not compute**, the recalculated allocation: the
  UI must display the server's recalculated per-invoice payment allocation (and
  the resulting status each invoice would land in — PAID/SETTLED/PARTIAL) **before
  the user gives final confirmation**, so the settlement is never confirmed blind
  to what the server actually computed. The client's own arithmetic, if any is
  shown optimistically while typing, is never authoritative and is always replaced
  by the server's recalculation before confirmation.

### 7. Settlement-screen UI contract — binding, not a suggestion

This behavior is a **binding future POS/receivables UX contract**, locked now so
Phase 3 design does not re-litigate it:

- Default state (Manual Allocation = OFF, Settlement Discount = OFF): the detailed
  per-invoice allocation table is **hidden**; only a simple settlement summary is
  shown (amount received, invoices it will clear, in FIFO order).
- The detailed table becomes visible whenever **either** toggle is ON:
  - **AUTO + Discount OFF** → table hidden.
  - **MANUAL + Discount OFF** → table visible; Payment column editable; Discount
    column disabled/read-only.
  - **AUTO + Discount ON** → table visible; Payment allocation is
    system-calculated/read-only (§6's recalculation, shown before confirmation);
    Discount column editable.
  - **MANUAL + Discount ON** → table visible; both Payment and Discount columns
    editable, each still subject to its own permission check (a user may be
    permitted to allocate manually but not permitted to apply a settlement
    discount, or vice versa — two independent permissions, not one combined
    toggle-permission).

### 8. Payment receipt vs. payment allocation — no double-counting

A **payment receipt** and a **payment allocation** are different events and must
never both reduce the customer's receivables for the same money:

```
Payment Receipt (money actually received from the customer)
   → Unapplied / Available Customer Amount (a pool, not yet tied to any invoice)
      → Invoice Allocation(s) (some or all of that available amount applied to
                                one or more specific invoices)
```

- A `PAYMENT` entry records **money received**, increasing the customer's
  **unapplied/available amount** — it does **not**, by itself, reduce any
  invoice's outstanding. Receiving a payment with nothing yet allocated leaves
  every invoice's outstanding untouched and the customer holding an available
  balance.
- A `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION` entry records **applying** some or all of that available
  amount to a specific invoice, reducing **that invoice's** outstanding by the
  allocated amount and **consuming** the same amount from the available pool.
- **The two are never summed together as if independent** — `current_outstanding`
  (§1) and any invoice's `outstanding` are derived from allocations
  (`PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`, plus §11's `ADVANCE_APPLIED`, minus discounts/write-downs)
  applied **against invoices**, never from raw `PAYMENT` totals directly. A
  `PAYMENT` that is never allocated reduces no invoice's outstanding at all — it
  only grows the available/advance pool (§11).
- **Atomicity is an implementation choice, not a conceptual shortcut.** If a
  payment is fully allocated at the moment it is received (the common POS case —
  pay exactly what is owed, immediately applied), the `PAYMENT` and its
  `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`(s) may be written in the same transaction, effectively
  atomic from the user's perspective. **The conceptual distinction and the
  reconciliation requirement (§15) still apply** — the implementation must still
  be able to show the payment receipt and its allocation(s) as separate,
  individually-reconcilable ledger entries, even when they happen together.

### 9. Settlement is a transaction/grouping concept, not a balance-affecting entry

`SETTLEMENT` is a **header/workflow/audit reference**, not an additional
balance-affecting ledger amount stacked on top of its component entries. A
settlement groups and orchestrates the effects of one collection event, which may
include any combination of: a payment receipt (§8), one or more payment
allocations (§8), a settlement discount (§5), an advance application (§11), and —
where applicable — a credit note / write-off. **The actual monetary/receivable
effects are carried entirely by those component entries** (`PAYMENT`,
`PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`, `SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT`, `ADVANCE_APPLIED`, `CREDIT_NOTE`,
`WRITE_OFF`) — summing a settlement's component entries must equal exactly what
moved; there is no separate `SETTLEMENT`-kind amount to add or reconcile on top. A
`settlement` header row (id, customer, initiated-by, timestamp, the toggle states
in effect, references to its component entries) is stored for workflow, audit, and
history — "which entries were processed together, by whom, under which mode" — but
it carries **no independent value field that participates in outstanding/balance
arithmetic**. This directly prevents the double-counting risk of treating
`SETTLEMENT` as a thirteenth kind of money movement alongside its own components.

### 10. Customer ledger / subledger — an append-only event taxonomy, not a mutable balance

`current_outstanding` and `advance_balance` (§1) are **derived projections**, never
the primary store — the same "ledger, not a mutable balance" discipline already
mandatory for inventory (`branch_inventory_balance`) and cash (`cash_movement`
sessions). The customer subledger (`ar_transaction`/`advance_transaction`, already
named in `DOMAIN-MODEL.md`) carries this entry-kind taxonomy:

`INVOICE · PAYMENT · PAYMENT_ALLOCATION · SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT · CREDIT_NOTE ·
ADVANCE · ADVANCE_APPLIED · REFUND · WRITE_OFF · ADJUSTMENT · REVERSAL`

(`SETTLEMENT` is deliberately **not** in this list — per §9, it is a header
concept referencing a group of the above entries, not itself an entry kind that
carries value.) The ledger must make it structurally impossible to confuse:

- **actual money received but not yet applied** (`PAYMENT`, per §8, before
  allocation) from
- **actual money applied to reduce a specific invoice** (`PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`) from
- **non-cash reductions** (`SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT`, `CREDIT_NOTE`, `WRITE_OFF`,
  `ADJUSTMENT`) from
- **customer-side balances that are not revenue at all** (`ADVANCE` /
  `ADVANCE_APPLIED`) from
- **money returned** (`REFUND`) from
- **corrections** (`REVERSAL`)

— each is its own entry kind, summed independently, never collapsed into one
"amount" column that could hide which of these actually moved cash versus merely
re-allocated or wrote down an existing obligation.

### 11. Advance / unallocated payment — never silently applied

If a payment received exceeds the amount allocated to invoices (whether under AUTO
FIFO, §6's recalculation, or manual allocation), the difference is **kept
explicitly unallocated** — it is not silently applied to some invoice the user did
not choose, and it does not silently become an advance either. Specifically:

- The unallocated remainder is shown clearly, as its own figure, on the settlement
  screen (§7) and in the settlement's record — never blended into an invoice's
  allocation.
- It becomes a customer **advance** (`ADVANCE`, applied later via `ADVANCE_APPLIED`
  against a future invoice) **only** through the tenant's approved advance flow —
  an explicit action, never an automatic side effect of an over-payment.
- The resulting `ADVANCE` entry **preserves a reference to the originating
  `PAYMENT`** — an advance is traceable back to the payment receipt that funded it,
  never a free-floating balance with no source event.
- Symmetrically, `ADVANCE_APPLIED` (an existing advance balance applied to reduce a
  new invoice's outstanding) counts as **actual monetary value** for the PAID
  determination (§2) — it is real money the customer already gave the business,
  simply applied later; it is not a settlement discount and does not make an
  invoice `SETTLED` by itself.

### 12. Correction / reversal — append-only, no destructive edits

Completed financial history is append-only, exactly matching the existing GL rule
(§F.1 "append-only... a correction is a reversing entry plus a new entry, never an
edit") and the Z-Report immutability rule (CLAUDE.md rule 19) — extended here to the
customer subledger: a completed payment, allocation, or settlement discount is
**never** directly edited or deleted. A correction is a `REVERSAL` entry plus a
new, corrected entry (a new `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`, `SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT`, etc., as
appropriate — never a new `SETTLEMENT`-kind entry, per §9). Invoice payment status
and outstanding are **always recomputed from the authoritative event stream**
(§15), never patched directly — if the events say outstanding is X, the
status/outstanding fields reflect X; there is no code path that sets status
without deriving it from events.

### 13. Discount types — kept structurally separate

Four distinct discount/reduction concepts, never conflated in schema, code, or
reporting:

1. **Line-item sale discount** — applied to an order line at sale time (existing
   contra-revenue mechanism, ZF-7).
2. **Invoice/order discount** — applied to the whole order at sale time (same
   contra-revenue mechanism, order-level instead of line-level).
3. **Promotion/coupon** — a rule-driven discount at sale time (Phase 7/10 scope,
   unchanged by this ADR).
4. **Settlement discount** (new, §5) — applied **after** the sale, against an
   already-posted receivable, to close it out. Structurally and temporally
   distinct from 1–3: it never touches the original invoice/order total (§5), it
   has its own approval/audit shape (§5), and it posts to its own account (§14),
   never to the point-of-sale Discounts (contra-revenue) account.

### 14. GL / accounting — receivables reconcile to the GL, invoice never mutated

Receivables continue to reconcile to the GL exactly as already designed (§F.11):
the customer subledger (§10) is detail; the GL's AR control account is the
summary; they must always tie. A settlement with a discount posts (conceptually;
exact account keys are a Phase 3 posting-template detail, not fixed here) — note
the journal entry is composed from the **component entries** (`PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`

- `SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT`), per §9, not from a separate `SETTLEMENT` amount:

```
AR                              500
Customer pays                   450
Settlement discount              50

Dr Cash/Bank                    450       (from the PAYMENT / PAYMENT_ALLOCATION)
Dr Settlement Discount /
   Approved Adjustment           50       (from the SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT entry)
Cr Accounts Receivable          500
```

matching the existing idempotent, `source_kind+source_id`-keyed, balanced-entry
posting discipline (ZF-1, F.1) — the component subledger entries and their journal
entries commit in the same transaction as the settlement they belong to, exactly
like every other posting-worthy event already in the design; the `settlement`
header (§9) is not itself a posting source. **The original posted invoice/order is
never mutated** to force its balance to zero — the invoice's `total` stays exactly
what was charged; only `outstanding` (derived, §10/§15) moves. Exact account keys
and per-jurisdiction tax treatment are a Phase 3 posting-template design detail,
not fixed by this ADR. Where fiscal/tax law requires a credit note or equivalent
statutory document for a receivable write-down (as it already may for KSA under
Z-8/ADR-0012's ZATCA gate), the settlement-discount posting integrates with the
same country-specific `EInvoicingProvider` adapter port and the same
KSA-onboarding gate already approved — this ADR does not add a second, separate
fiscal gate.

### 15. Reconciliation invariant

For any invoice (and, summed across a customer's invoices, for the customer as a
whole), the current outstanding must always be **provable** from the authoritative
subledger event stream, never from a manually edited balance:

```
outstanding
  = original invoice obligation
  − Σ payment allocations (PAYMENT_ALLOCATION)
  − Σ advance applied (ADVANCE_APPLIED)
  − Σ settlement discounts (SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT)
  − Σ credit notes / approved write-downs (CREDIT_NOTE, WRITE_OFF)
  + Σ reversals / valid adjustments (REVERSAL, ADJUSTMENT — signed so a REVERSAL
    of a prior reduction adds back, never subtracts twice)
```

This is the same discipline as `branch_inventory_balance` being provable from
`inventory_movement`, and the GL being provable from its journal entries — a
reconciliation job (mirroring the existing nightly inventory-balance
reconciliation, §16·20·21) can and should recompute `outstanding` from this
formula and alert on drift, exactly like the existing Inventory-control-account
reconciliation (§F.10). No stored `outstanding`/`current_outstanding` value is ever
trusted over what this formula derives.

### 16. Transactional integrity — reuses the existing outbox/transaction pattern, no new mechanism

Payment receipt, allocation(s), the resulting receivable effects, settlement
discount + its approval reference, the customer-ledger entries, and any required
audit records commit **together, in one DB transaction**, exactly matching the
already-approved pattern for stock+GL+cash-movement+order-state (CLAUDE.md rule 29) and for inventory↔GL postings (§F.10). External side effects (a receipt email,
a WhatsApp payment confirmation, a statement notification) stay **outside** that
transaction and go through the existing transactional outbox (CLAUDE.md rule 28),
unchanged. No new transactional mechanism is introduced by this ADR.

## Part B — Cancellation, refund, customer account credit & cancellation charge

**Added 2026-09-05, same day, before first commit — owner review round 3.**
Extends Part A (§1–§16) with the cancellation/refund/account-credit lifecycle
those sections assumed but did not specify. No section of Part A is reversed;
Part B cross-references Part A's entry taxonomy (§10), reconciliation invariant
(§15), and transactional pattern (§16) rather than duplicating them.

### 17. Delete vs. cancel vs. financial reversal — the right undo mechanism depends on lifecycle stage

Destructive delete is **never** used for a financially meaningful transaction.
The correct undo mechanism depends on how far the transaction has progressed:

- **DRAFT / never confirmed, never posted** — delete may be permitted, gated by
  permission and audited, exactly like any other draft-stage edit elsewhere in
  the design (e.g. a `DRAFT`/review-state catalog item, §15·17·18·19).
- **CONFIRMED but not yet financially posted** (no invoice/payment/receivable
  effect exists yet) — **cancel**, a state transition, not a row deletion.
- **INVOICED / PAID / PARTIAL / CREDIT / SETTLED** (any state where a financial
  or receivable effect already exists) — **never** a destructive delete. Only a
  cancellation / void / refund / credit / reversal workflow (§19 onward) may
  unwind it, and completed financial history remains append-only exactly as
  already required for the GL (§F.1) and the customer subledger (§10/§12):
  corrections are **reversal + corrected transaction**, never an edit or an
  erasure.

### 18. Separate lifecycle states — six independent state machines, never collapsed

At least six conceptually distinct lifecycles exist on the same underlying
order/invoice/customer, and are **never** collapsed into one status field:

1. **Order status** — the existing fulfilment state machine (`DRAFT/HELD → … →
COMPLETED/DELIVERED` + `REJECTED · CANCELLED · PAYMENT_FAILED · REFUNDED ·
DELIVERY_FAILED · RESCHEDULED`, `DOMAIN-MODEL.md`'s Order aggregate,
   unchanged). **Clarification, not an alteration**: where that already-frozen
   state machine's own `REFUNDED`/`PAYMENT_FAILED` values are used, they are a
   **permitted coarse projection** for order-level display/reporting — e.g. "this
   order's story involved a refund" — never the authoritative source for refund
   detail. The authoritative refund state lives in Refund Status (5, below); the
   order's own field is not re-derived from it record-by-record and is not
   contradicted by treating it as a summary flag.
2. **Invoice payment status** — §2 (`UNPAID → PARTIAL → {PAID | SETTLED} ·
PARTIALLY_REFUNDED → REFUNDED · CANCELLED/VOID`), unchanged.
3. **Payment status** — the existing per-payment-transaction state machine
   (`REQUIRES_ACTION → AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED → PARTIALLY_REFUNDED → REFUNDED` ·
   `FAILED · CANCELED · PENDING`, §42·43), unchanged — one payment transaction's
   own state, distinct from the invoice it was allocated to.
4. **Receivable status** — a **derived, informational** view of a customer's
   aggregate position, computed from §15's reconciliation invariant, not a
   stored field with its own writes: `CLEAR` (no outstanding, no advance),
   `OUTSTANDING` (net amount due), `ADVANCE_HELD` (net credit available), or
   `MIXED` (both an outstanding invoice and an unapplied advance exist
   simultaneously — §20's example: Receivable 300 + Advance 500 shown as a net
   position, both balances still tracked independently underneath).
5. **Refund status** — new: `NONE → PENDING → {COMPLETED | PARTIAL | FAILED} ·
REVERSED`, attached to a specific refund/account-credit resolution (§19),
   tracking that resolution's own progress (a gateway refund can be genuinely
   asynchronous/pending; an account-credit resolution completes synchronously).
6. **Inventory return/consumption/disposition status** — new, per affected
   order line: `PENDING_DISPOSITION → {RESERVATION_RELEASED | RETURNED_TO_STOCK |
FINISHED_GOOD_STOCK_IN | REUSABLE_COMPONENT_RETURN | WASTAGE | SPOILAGE |
SCRAP | other configured disposition}` (§28) — physical state, never inferred
   from the financial cancellation alone (§29).

A single order can legitimately show, at once: Order Status `CANCELLED`,
Invoice Status `REFUNDED` or `CANCELLED/VOID`, one Payment's own status
`PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, Refund Status `COMPLETED`, and per-line Inventory
Disposition Status varying line by line — exactly the "order CANCELLED while
payment resolution is REFUNDED or CREDITED_TO_ACCOUNT" example given, generalized
to all six axes.

### 19. Cancellation monetary resolution — refund, account credit, or split; validated against actual money received

On cancellation (full or partial, §21), the customer's monetary resolution is
computed **only from actual monetary value already received on the cancelled
portion** (§8's `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION` history via the reconciliation invariant,
§15) — never from the invoice's nominal total:

- **Fully paid, fully cancelled** (paid = total): the full paid amount is
  eligible for resolution, split freely across refund and/or account credit
  (§20's example: paid 500, cancel all → 500 eligible).
- **Partially paid, fully cancelled** (paid < total): only the amount **actually
  received** on the cancelled portion is eligible — the still-**unpaid**
  remainder of the obligation is simply cancelled/reversed, never refunded (it
  was never money) (§21's example: paid 200 of 500 → only 200 eligible, the
  unpaid 300 is reversed with zero refund).
- **Unpaid, cancelled** (paid = 0): the receivable obligation is
  cancelled/reversed; the refundable/creditable amount is exactly zero — unless
  a cancellation charge applies (§23) and enforceable, in which case a **new**,
  separate fee receivable may result (never a reduction of the original,
  already-cancelled 0-payment invoice) (§22's example).
- **Account credit is not a refund.** If money does not leave the business, the
  resolved amount is **never** recorded as a `REFUND` — it becomes an `ADVANCE`
  entry (§10; see §26 for why this reuses `ADVANCE` rather than a parallel
  entry kind), immediately available for the customer to apply to a future
  invoice via the existing `ADVANCE_APPLIED` mechanism, exactly as already
  specified in §1/§11 (§20's example: paid 500, cancelled, customer chooses
  account credit → `REFUND = 0`, `ADVANCE = 500`; a later invoice for 700 then
  applies `ADVANCE_APPLIED = 500` + a new `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION = 200` →
  outstanding 0).
- **Split resolution** — a single refundable/creditable pool may be resolved
  across **multiple destinations** in one cancellation (e.g. Cash/Card Refund
  200 + Account Credit 300 from a 500 pool). Server-validated: the sum of every
  resolution component **never exceeds** the eligible refundable/creditable
  amount computed above; each component (a `REFUND` toward one destination, an
  `ADVANCE` toward account credit) is its own separately-auditable,
  separately-reconcilable ledger entry — never one blended "resolution" amount.
- **The original payment is never mutated or deleted** (§17) — refund/credit
  resolution is new `REFUND`/`ADVANCE` entries referencing the original
  `PAYMENT`/`PAYMENT_ALLOCATION` they resolve against, exactly matching §8's
  append-only, non-double-counting discipline.

### 20. Partial item / partial order cancellation — line-level financial and inventory reversal

Cancellation is **not required to be whole-order**. Where business/fiscal rules
allow, a single line (or a subset of lines) may be cancelled while the rest of
the order/invoice remains intact under the existing sales/fiscal model
(§20/§22's Bouquet 200 + Perfume 300 example: cancelling only the Perfume yields
a cancelled value of 300, resolved by refund/credit/split, while the Bouquet
line's invoice/receivable/inventory state is untouched). This requires:

- the cancellation event to reference the **specific order line(s)** cancelled,
  not only the order/invoice as a whole;
- the monetary resolution (§19) to be computed against **only the cancelled
  line(s)' actual paid value**, via the same reconciliation-invariant approach,
  never the whole invoice's paid total;
- the surviving lines' invoice/payment/receivable state to be **entirely
  unaffected** by the partial cancellation — no re-derivation that could
  perturb an already-settled or already-paid sibling line;
- inventory disposition (§28) to apply **only** to the cancelled line(s)'
  reservation/consumption, never touching the surviving lines' stock effects.

### 21. Cancellation charge — a first-class financial concept, never a silent refund reduction

A **cancellation charge** is its own first-class concept — never a silent
reduction folded invisibly into a smaller refund number. Every cancellation
charge is a separate, recorded entry:

`CANCELLATION_CHARGE` (a fee retained by, or owed to, the business as a
consequence of the cancellation policy) and its reversal, `CANCELLATION_CHARGE_
REVERSAL` (an override or correction — §25 — undoing a charge, itself append-only
per §17).

Worked example (§21): Invoice 500, Paid 500, Cancellation Charge 50 →
Refundable/Creditable = 450, with the `CANCELLATION_CHARGE = 50` entry recorded
**separately** and visible on every relevant view (§29), never merely subtracted
silently from "the refund."

### 22. Cancellation charge policy — configurable, never hard-coded

Cancellation-charge **policy** is tenant-configurable, not hard-coded into the
core, matching the platform's general "extensible concepts are configuration,
never a code branch" discipline (ADR-0018 §6's identical rule for catalog
behaviour, applied here to financial policy). Supported policy shapes (examples
only — the exact tenant-configured rule set is a Phase 3 design/data concern,
never a fixed list in code): no charge; a fixed amount; a percentage; a minimum
charge; a time-based charge (e.g. by elapsed time since confirmation); a
stage-based charge (e.g. by order/production stage reached — before
confirmation / after confirmation / preparation started / custom item produced
/ out for delivery); a non-refundable deposit or component, where
legally/business valid. The percentages and stages given anywhere in this ADR
(0% / 10% / 30% / 50% / a configured non-refundable amount) are **illustrative
examples only** — never literal defaults baked into code.

**Calculation base must be explicit, never ambiguous.** A policy must state
which base it calculates against — e.g. the net invoice payable amount, the
cancelled line's net amount, a configured deposit, or an explicitly designated
non-refundable amount. **Recommended generic default** (a starting point for
Phase 3 design, not a locked value): net eligible amount after normal sale
discounts (ZF-7), subject to jurisdiction and tenant policy. The calculated
charge is never computed against an arbitrary mutable total — enough
information (which policy version, which base amount, which stage/time/percent
was applied) is stored **with the `CANCELLATION_CHARGE` entry itself** so the
charge is reproducible from stored data alone, exactly matching the recipe
versioning precedent already in the design (§23·24·25·26's "historical orders
reference the recipe version in force at order time").

### 23. Cancellation charge interacting with the amount actually paid

The cancellation charge is always evaluated **against actual money received**,
never against the original nominal total, mirroring §19's rule:

- **Partial payment + charge, charge ≤ paid** (§23's own example: Invoice 500,
  Paid 200, Charge 50 → the unpaid 300 obligation is simply reversed — it was
  never money; of the 200 actually received, 50 is retained as the charge,
  leaving 150 refundable/creditable). The unpaid portion is **never** treated as
  money received merely because a charge applies to it.
- **Charge exceeds amount paid** (Invoice 500, Paid 20, Charge 50): the original
  500 sale obligation is still fully cancelled/reversed; the charge exceeds the
  20 actually received, so the **shortfall (30) may become a separate,
  standalone cancellation-fee receivable**, if policy/legal rules allow it to be
  enforced — the original, already-cancelled product invoice is **never**
  mutated into a smaller "AED 50 invoice" to represent this; a separate
  charge/fee-document/debit-note concept (§27) carries it.
- **Unpaid order + enforceable charge** (Invoice 500, Paid 0, Charge 50): the
  500 obligation is reversed/cancelled with refund = 0; if enforceable, a
  standalone `CANCELLATION_CHARGE`-backed fee receivable of 50 is created via
  the same separate fee-document concept — again, never a mutation of the
  original invoice.

### 24. Cancellation charge override — authorized, audited, never a silent overwrite

An authorized manager/owner may override a calculated cancellation charge where
tenant policy permits (e.g. Calculated 50 → Final 25, reason "goodwill"). The
override **never overwrites the calculation** — the audit trail preserves, at
minimum: the original calculated charge, the policy/rule version used, the
final (overridden) charge, the override reason, applied-by, approved-by (where
policy requires a distinct approver from the person applying it — mirroring
§5's settlement-discount approval separation), tenant/company/branch/POS
context, and a timestamp — the identical shape already required for a
settlement discount (§5) and a custom-bouquet margin override (Z-9), applied
here to the same class of decision.

### 25. Settled-invoice cancellation — reverse the receivable AND the settlement effect, never refund the waived amount

Cancelling a `SETTLED` invoice (§2) — one already brought to zero outstanding
using payment **plus** a settlement discount — requires **all** of:

1. reversing/cancelling the original receivable obligation (§17's reversal
   discipline);
2. reversing the settlement-discount effect that had been applied to it (a
   `REVERSAL` entry against the original `SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT`, per §12) — the
   discount was never money, so there is nothing to "give back" through it, only
   to correctly unwind from the ledger's perspective;
3. calculating any applicable cancellation charge (§21–§23), against the
   ACTUAL paid amount;
4. resolving only the **actual monetary value / advance** the customer is
   entitled to (§19) — the worked example (§25): original invoice 500, actual
   payment 450, settlement discount 50 → **the refundable/creditable base is
   450 (the actual payment), never 500 and never including the waived 50**;
5. preserving the full chain of history (§30) — nothing here is a destructive
   edit to the original `INVOICE`/`SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT` entries.

### 26. Discount/charge taxonomy — six structurally separate concepts, extending §13

§13's four discount concepts are extended to six, all structurally separate in
schema, code, and reporting, never combined into one generic mutable "discount"
amount (each occurs at a different lifecycle stage with different accounting,
tax, audit and approval implications):

1. Line-item sale discount (§13.1, unchanged).
2. Invoice/order sale discount (§13.2, unchanged).
3. Promotion/coupon (§13.3, unchanged).
4. Settlement discount (§13.4/§5, unchanged) — reduces a still-open receivable.
5. **Cancellation charge** (§21, new) — a fee arising from cancelling an
   already-invoiced (paid or unpaid) obligation; posts to its own account
   (§27), distinct from every discount above.
6. **Cancellation-charge override** (§24, new) — not a seventh amount, but the
   audited adjustment path for concept 5, kept structurally distinct from an
   ordinary settlement-discount approval even though the shapes are similar.

Write-offs and credit notes (already in §10's taxonomy) remain their own
concepts too, distinct from all six above.

### 27. Refund methods, provider adapters, and refund validation

**Refund/resolution methods** — supported where enabled per tenant/payment
configuration: refund to the original payment method, cash refund, card
refund, bank-transfer refund, payment-gateway refund, customer account
credit/advance (§19), and split resolution (§19) across any combination of the
above. **Provider-specific refund APIs stay behind the existing
`PaymentProvider` adapter port** (§42·43's `createIntent · authorize · capture
· refund · getStatus · verifyWebhook`) — the refund/cancellation domain never
hard-codes provider logic, exactly matching the existing adapter-isolation rule
(Z-4/ADR-0007).

**Refund validation is computed from authoritative financial history, never
from a mutable "paid" field.** The refundable amount for any given resolution
accounts for: actual payment received (§8), previous refunds, previous
account-credit resolutions, any cancellation charge (§21–§23), settlement
adjustment reversals (§25), credit notes/write-downs where applicable, prior
partial cancellations (§20), and prior reversals (§12/§17) — i.e. the full
reconciliation invariant (§15/§28), never a single stored balance. **The system
never refunds more monetary value than the customer actually provided**, except
through a separately authorized compensation/goodwill flow (which is itself an
explicit, audited, approved entry — e.g. a manager-approved goodwill credit —
never an accidental over-refund from a stale balance).

### 28. Customer account credit / advance reconciliation — two balances, never destructively netted

Customer Account Credit (an `ADVANCE`, per §26 below) reconciles to the same
`advance_transaction` subledger already named in §10/§F.11, and preserves links
to: the originating `PAYMENT`, the originating cancelled/refunded
order/invoice, the cancellation transaction that created it, any associated
`CANCELLATION_CHARGE`, later `ADVANCE_APPLIED` entries against it, and a refund
of the unused advance if one is later performed (a customer may ask for a
still-unapplied account credit to be paid back out — that is itself a new
`REFUND` entry referencing the `ADVANCE`, not a mutation of it).

**Customer Outstanding Receivable and Customer Advance remain separate
balances, always.** A UI may display a convenience net position (§20's example:
Receivable 300, Advance 500 → UI shows "Due 300 / Advance 500 / Net Position
200 Credit"), but the two subledger balances are **never destructively netted**
in storage — both remain independently derivable from the reconciliation
invariant (§15) at all times.

### 29. Inventory consequences of cancellation — physical disposition is a separate concern from the financial reversal

Financial cancellation and physical inventory disposition are **separate
concerns**, resolved independently:

- A reservation that was **held but never consumed** → released
  (`stock_reservation` → `RELEASED`, the existing §22 lifecycle, unchanged).
- A **standard, unopened retail item** already handed over and returned →
  `CUSTOMER_RETURN` (already an existing `inventory_movement` category, §16·20·21)
  restoring eligible stock/batch/lot.
- A **bouquet, cake, or other already-produced BOM/custom item** → raw
  materials already consumed via `MATERIAL_CONSUMPTION` (§23·24·25·26) are
  **not** automatically restored — cancelling the sale does not un-consume
  flowers already cut and assembled. Disposition of the finished item itself
  (and any reusable components) is decided per tenant policy and the item's
  actual physical state, modeled via a disposition outcome such as:
  `RETURN_TO_STOCK` (a still-sellable finished good), `FINISHED_GOOD_STOCK_IN`,
  `REUSABLE_COMPONENT_RETURN` (an unopened, reusable component recovered from
  the composition), `WASTAGE`, `SPOILAGE`, or `SCRAP`/another configured
  disposition — always through the existing single inventory movement engine
  (§16·20·21), branch-scoped and append-only, never a parallel mechanism.

### 30. Cancellation charge never controls inventory disposition

A cancellation charge is a **financial** policy outcome; it never automatically
decides physical inventory disposition. "Cancellation Charge = AED 50" does
**not** mean "consume AED 50 worth of stock," and no code path derives an
inventory movement quantity or category from a charge amount. Inventory
disposition (§29) is decided from the item's actual physical state and material
consumption record, entirely independent of what financial charge, if any, was
applied to the cancellation.

### 31. Customer ledger / subledger — extended taxonomy, still non-double-counting

§10's taxonomy is extended for cancellation/refund/account-credit, while
preserving every non-double-counting rule already locked in §8/§9/§10:

`INVOICE · PAYMENT · PAYMENT_ALLOCATION · SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT · CREDIT_NOTE ·
ADVANCE · ADVANCE_APPLIED · REFUND · WRITE_OFF · ADJUSTMENT · REVERSAL ·
CANCELLATION_CHARGE · CANCELLATION_CHARGE_REVERSAL`

Two clarifications, both deliberate, both closing an ambiguity the requesting
instruction itself flagged:

- **"Account Credit" is not a new, parallel entry kind.** It **is** an
  `ADVANCE` entry (§1/§11/§19) — distinguished from an over-payment-sourced
  advance only by its `source_kind` reference (e.g. `PAYMENT_OVERAGE` vs.
  `CANCELLATION_RESOLUTION` vs. a future `GOODWILL` reason), never a second
  balance-bearing concept that could double-count against `advance_balance`.
  This is the same "reuse the mechanism, distinguish by source reference"
  approach already used for `ADVANCE_APPLIED` counting toward PAID (§11).
- **`SETTLEMENT` remains excluded from this taxonomy** (§9, unchanged) — a
  cancellation's settlement-discount reversal (§25) is a `REVERSAL` entry
  against the original `SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT`, never a new `SETTLEMENT`-kind
  amount.
- As in §10, this is a **closed, reference-table-backed taxonomy**
  (`ARCHITECTURE.md`'s "extensible concepts are strings + reference tables, not
  PostgreSQL enums" convention), not necessarily a hardcoded database enum —
  Phase 3 implementation detail, not fixed here.

### 32. Refund / account-credit reversal — the full chain must be reconstructible

Completed refund/account-credit history is append-only (§17) — a wrong refund
or account-credit resolution is **never** edited or deleted; it is corrected via
an explicit reversal/correction flow with linked references (§12's pattern,
applied here). The system must be able to reconstruct the **entire** chain for
any invoice: `Original Invoice → Payment → Allocation → Cancellation →
Cancellation Charge → Refund / Account Credit → Reversal/Correction (if any)` —
purely by following reference links between subledger entries, never by
inference from a mutable summary field.

### 33. GL / accounting implications of cancellation, refund and account credit

Cancellation/refund/account-credit/cancellation-charge flows reconcile to the
GL exactly as §14 already requires for settlement. Conceptually (exact account
keys and jurisdiction-specific tax treatment remain a Phase 3
posting-template/fiscal-adapter detail, not fixed here):

- The original receivable's posting (`Dr AR / Cr Revenue+VAT`, §F.1, unchanged)
  is reversed according to the approved posting engine and applicable fiscal
  rules when the underlying sale is cancelled.
- A cancellation charge may post to a configured cancellation-fee income/
  adjustment account, per tenant policy and jurisdiction — a distinct account
  from Sales Revenue, Settlement Discount, or any other existing account, per
  §26's structural-separation rule.
- **Customer Account Credit creates/maintains a customer-advance liability
  balance** (the existing Customer Advances account, §F.1) — it is never
  recorded as revenue and never recorded as cash leaving the business; no
  journal entry for an account-credit resolution ever debits Cash/Bank.

### 34. Fiscal document implications

Depending on country/tax rules, a cancellation, refund, settlement, or
cancellation charge may require a specific statutory document — a credit note,
a debit note, a refund document, a cancellation document, a fee invoice, or an
equivalent instrument. **No single jurisdiction's document model is hard-coded
into the core** — this routes through the same existing country-specific
`EInvoicingProvider` adapter port (Z-4/ADR-0007) and the same
KSA-onboarding/ZATCA gate already approved (Z-8/ADR-0012, referenced in §14) —
this ADR introduces no second, parallel fiscal gate for cancellation/refund
documents.

### 35. Transaction/audit invariants for cancellation

Cancellation-related financial mutations preserve transactional integrity
exactly per §16 (one DB transaction for the domain effects; external side
effects via the existing outbox, CLAUDE.md rules 28–29) — no new transactional
mechanism. The required audit history additionally preserves, where
applicable: tenant/company/branch/POS, customer, order, invoice, the specific
cancelled line(s) (§20), the original payment(s), the cancellation policy
version applied, the calculated cancellation charge, any overridden charge +
override reason (§24), refund amount(s) and destination(s), the account-credit
amount, the settlement-discount reversal (§25), the inventory-disposition
reference (§29), applied-by, approved-by, timestamp, and reversal/correction
links (§32) — the same class of audit completeness already required for a
settlement discount (§5) and a custom-bouquet margin override (Z-9), extended
to this larger event set.

### 36. Reconciliation invariant — extended for cancellation/refund/account-credit

§15's invariant is extended, not replaced, into **three** distinct, separately
provable derivations — kept distinct deliberately, because "what is still owed,"
"how much of a specific cancellation is refundable," and "what advance/credit
balance exists" are different questions that must never be computed by one
merged formula (doing so is exactly the kind of ambiguity that risks
double-counting):

**(a) An invoice's outstanding** (unchanged in shape from §15, restated for
completeness) — what the customer still owes on this invoice, right now:

```
outstanding = original invoice obligation
  − Σ payment allocations
  − Σ advance applied
  − Σ settlement discounts
  − Σ credit notes / approved write-downs
  + Σ reversals / valid adjustments (signed so a REVERSAL of a prior
    reduction adds back, never subtracts twice)
```

A **valid cancellation** removes the (remaining, unpaid) obligation via a
reversal of this same kind — it does not need a separate term; it is simply
another reversal-shaped event in this formula, exactly like any other
correction (§12/§17).

**(b) A cancellation event's refundable/creditable amount** — a
narrower, event-scoped derivation, computed **once per cancellation**, over
**only that cancellation's** cancelled scope (the whole invoice, or the
specific line(s), §20):

```
refundable/creditable (for this cancellation) =
    Σ payment allocations actually applied to the cancelled scope
  + Σ advance applied to the cancelled scope
  − Σ cancellation charge(s) recorded against this cancellation
  − Σ refunds already resolved against this cancellation
  − Σ account-credit (ADVANCE) already created against this cancellation
```

This is never summed into, or netted against, quantity (a) — a cancellation's
refundable amount is resolved (§19) by creating new `REFUND`/`ADVANCE` entries
that themselves reduce this same formula's remaining balance to zero (the last
two terms exist specifically so a **second** attempt to resolve the same
cancellation cannot re-refund/re-credit an amount already resolved — the
"eligible remaining" for any further resolution is always this formula's
current value, never the original pool re-read from scratch).

**(c) A customer's advance/account-credit balance** (`advance_balance`, §1) —
unchanged in shape from the existing design, extended only in what can feed it:

```
advance_balance = Σ ADVANCE entries (from payment overages, §11, and/or from
                   cancellation resolutions, §19/§31 — both sources, same
                   entry kind, distinguished only by source reference)
  − Σ ADVANCE_APPLIED entries
  − Σ refunds of a still-unapplied advance (§28)
```

No stored `outstanding`/`refundable`/`advance_balance` value is ever trusted
over what these three formulas derive from the append-only event stream —
derived summaries may be cached for read performance, but the authoritative
subledger remains the source of truth and stays fully reconcilable, exactly as
§15 already requires for the simpler pre-cancellation case. **Because (a),
(b), and (c) each have their own formula and their own scope, no single
monetary event is ever counted against more than one of them in a way that
inflates the customer's or the business's position** — a `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`
reduces (a) once; if its invoice is later cancelled, that same allocation
appears in (b)'s cancellation-scoped sum, not as an additional reduction to
(a) beyond the reversal that already zeroed the remaining obligation; a
resulting `ADVANCE` appears in (c), not as a second credit in (b).

### 37. UI / POS cancellation flow — binding future UX intent

A future POS/Owner cancellation screen (not built by this ADR — a Phase 3 UX
requirement, locked now the same way §7 locked the settlement screen) must
expose, before the user confirms a cancellation: the original total, the actual
amount paid, the cancellation policy that applies and why (e.g. "Preparation
Started — 10%"), the calculated cancellation fee, the final cancellation fee
(if a permitted override changed it, §24), the resulting refundable/creditable
amount, and an explicit resolution selector (refund to original method / cash
or other permitted refund / customer account credit / split resolution) with a
reason field and manager-override/approval control where required. **The
cancellation charge is never hidden inside the refund amount** — every figure
above is shown as its own line, mirroring §2's "never collapse figures" rule
for PAID vs. SETTLED, applied here to the cancellation screen.

## Consequences

- **Phase 3's already-scoped `receivables`/`payments`/`accounting` modules gain
  concrete scope**, not new modules: the invoice payment-status state machine, the
  settlement/allocation workflow (auto-FIFO + manual toggle + deterministic
  ordering + AUTO/discount recalculation), the settlement discount workflow
  (toggle + approval), the customer-ledger entry taxonomy (with the
  payment-vs-allocation and settlement-as-header clarifications), and the
  settlement-screen UI contract are all additive detail within modules already
  planned for that phase.
- **New schema, deferred to Phase 3 design, not specified in full here**:
  `invoice_payment_status` (or an equivalent computed field on `order`/a future
  dedicated `invoice` entity — whether "invoice" is the `order` itself or a
  separate entity is a Phase 3 modeling decision, not fixed by this ADR),
  `settlement` (header only, per §9 — no value column that participates in
  balance arithmetic), `payment` + `payment_allocation` (one payment, many invoice
  allocations, per §8), `settlement_discount` (per-invoice-per-settlement, with
  reason/approval/audit columns per §5), and the extended `ar_transaction`
  entry-kind taxonomy (§10).
- **New posting templates** for `PAYMENT_ALLOCATION`/`SETTLEMENT_DISCOUNT` extend
  §F.1's table; no existing posting template row is changed. There is no separate
  `SETTLEMENT` posting template, per §9/§14.
- **New permissions** (exact keys deferred to Phase 3 planning, not fixed here):
  distinct permissions for manual payment allocation, settlement-discount entry,
  and settlement-discount approval — three separate capabilities, not one
  "settlement:manage" catch-all, so a tenant can grant allocation without
  discount authority or vice versa (§7's "each still subject to its own permission
  check").
- **No change to the `order` fulfilment or `payment` state machines** — invoice
  payment status is a third, independent state machine layered alongside them, not
  a replacement or a merge.
- **A reconciliation job is now an explicit requirement** (§15), mirroring the
  existing inventory-balance and AR-control-account reconciliation jobs already in
  the design (§F.10/§F.11) — not a new class of mechanism, an application of the
  existing one to the customer subledger.

**Part B adds (§17–§37):**

- **Six independent lifecycle state machines** (§18) must be modeled from Phase
  3's first pass — Order, Invoice, Payment, Receivable (derived), Refund, and
  Inventory-disposition status — never collapsed into fewer fields.
- **New schema, deferred to Phase 3 design**: a `cancellation` event/record
  (referencing specific order line(s), §20), `cancellation_charge` (+ its
  reversal, +policy/base/version snapshot per §22), a standalone
  cancellation-fee document concept distinct from the original invoice (§23),
  and the extended `ar_transaction` taxonomy (§31, incl. `CANCELLATION_CHARGE`/
  `CANCELLATION_CHARGE_REVERSAL`).
- **"Account Credit" is explicitly modeled as an `ADVANCE` with a source
  reference** (§31), not a parallel balance-bearing concept — closing the
  ambiguity the requesting instruction itself raised.
- **New posting templates** for cancellation-charge income/adjustment and for
  reversing a cancelled sale's original posting extend §F.1's table (§33); no
  existing posting template row is changed.
- **New permissions** (exact keys deferred to Phase 3): cancellation itself,
  cancellation-charge override, and refund/account-credit resolution are
  independent capabilities, following the same separation-of-capabilities
  pattern as §7's settlement toggles.
- **A cancellation-charge policy engine is new scope** (§22) — configurable
  rules (fixed/percentage/minimum/time-based/stage-based/non-refundable
  component), evaluated against an explicit, storable calculation base, never
  hard-coded percentages.

## Non-goals (explicitly out of scope for this ADR)

- No schema, migration, API, or UI is implemented by this ADR.
- No exact account keys, exact permission keys, or exact `settlement`/
  `payment_allocation`/`settlement_discount` column lists are finalized — Phase 3
  planning deliverables.
- No decision on whether "invoice" becomes a dedicated entity distinct from
  `order` — left to Phase 3 modeling, framed but not resolved here.
- No change to Phase 2-core's remaining scope (Task 2.7, Task 2.8).
- Does not re-open or alter promotions/coupons (Phase 7/10, unchanged), gift cards
  (already named in the Phase 3 `receivables` module scope, unchanged by this
  ADR), or the e-invoicing/ZATCA gate (Z-8/ADR-0012, referenced not altered).

**Part B additionally does not:**

- Fix exact cancellation-charge policy values, stages, or percentages anywhere
  — every number in §22–§23 is illustrative only, per explicit instruction.
- Decide whether "invoice," "cancellation," and "cancellation charge" are
  separate entities or facets of a shared document model — a Phase 3 modeling
  decision, framed (a standalone fee-document concept, §23/§27) but not fixed.
- Alter the existing `inventory_movement` category set or the single inventory
  engine (§16·20·21/ADR-0018) — §29/§30 reuse it as-is.
- Change how `PaymentProvider` adapters work (§27) — refunds route through the
  existing port, unchanged.

## Risks / conflicts

**Conflicts with current architecture: none found.** Every posting template this
ADR extends already exists in §F.1 in the direction this ADR takes it (credit sale,
repayment, advance already model AR correctly); no existing decision is
contradicted.

**Schema / migration risks:**

1. **Invoice payment status must be designed into Phase 3's `order`/`payments`
   schema from the start**, not retrofitted — like ADR-0018's per-UOM-pricing gap,
   retrofitting a derived-status column plus a settlement/allocation schema after
   orders already exist in production would need a backfill and a change to every
   order-detail/reporting read path. Low risk if designed in from Phase 3's first
   pass (recommended), moderate if deferred.
2. **`payment` and `payment_allocation` must be separate rows/tables from the
   start** (§8) — a single-table shortcut that conflates "received" and "applied"
   would need a breaking schema split later, plus a data migration to
   retroactively separate historical rows. This is the highest-value gap this
   revision closes, and the one likeliest to be gotten wrong under time pressure
   if not called out explicitly at Phase 3 kickoff.
3. **`settlement` must ship as a header-only table from the start** (§9) — adding a
   balance-affecting `amount`/`total` column to it later, then having to prove no
   double-counting bug shipped in between, is a materially worse migration path
   than simply never adding that column.
4. **The customer subledger's entry-kind taxonomy (§10) should be a closed,
   reference-table-backed enum** (matching `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s existing "extensible
   concepts are strings + reference tables, not PostgreSQL enums" convention,
   `DOMAIN-MODEL.md` Conventions), not a hardcoded database enum — consistent with
   how `inventory_movement.category` and `payment` states are already modeled.
5. **Three-way state-machine independence (order / payment / invoice-payment-status)
   risks drift if not modeled carefully** — Phase 3 design must make invoice
   payment status a pure function of the subledger event stream (§15), never a
   field that can independently disagree with the ledger, the same discipline
   already required of `branch_inventory_balance` vs. the inventory movement
   ledger.
6. **The AUTO+discount recalculation (§6) must be a single, pure, deterministic
   function** re-run on every discount edit, not an incremental patch — an
   incremental "adjust the previous allocation" approach risks compounding
   rounding/ordering errors across edits; recomputing from scratch against the
   current invoice set on every change is safer and matches how the FIFO ordering
   itself (§3) is already defined as deterministic over current state, not
   incremental.
7. **No RLS/tenant-isolation risk identified** — the customer ledger, settlements,
   and invoice payment status are ordinary tenant- and branch-scoped rows, using
   the same `ScopedRepository`/RLS pattern as every other operational table; no new
   isolation boundary is introduced.
8. **No conflict with Phase 2-core** (Tasks 2.0–2.6, complete) — this ADR does not
   touch `apps/realtime`, `apps/worker`, the outbox schema, or the idempotency
   store; settlement writes will use the existing outbox/idempotency machinery
   once Phase 3 implements them, per §16.

**Part B (cancellation/refund/account-credit) risks:**

9. **Conflicts with current architecture: none found.** The existing Order state
   machine's `REFUNDED`/`PAYMENT_FAILED` values (`DOMAIN-MODEL.md`) are not
   altered — §18 only clarifies that they are a coarse projection, never the
   authoritative refund source, resolving a latent ambiguity rather than
   contradicting the existing field.
10. **"Account Credit" reusing `ADVANCE` (§31) must be designed in from Phase
    3's first schema pass** — a parallel `account_credit` balance/table added
    later, then discovered to double-count against `advance_balance`, would be
    a materially worse migration than simply reusing `ADVANCE` with a
    `source_kind` reference from day one (this is the same class of risk as
    Part A's `payment`/`payment_allocation` split, risk 2 above).
11. **The standalone cancellation-fee document concept (§23/§27) must be
    designed alongside the `invoice`/`accounting`/fiscal-adapter modules from
    the start** — retrofitting "a fee that isn't the original invoice" after
    invoice documents are already fiscally issued in production is a
    materially harder migration (potentially touching issued statutory
    documents) than designing for it up front.
12. **The six-way lifecycle-state independence (§18) is the highest-value
    design discipline in Part B** — a single overloaded status field is the
    single most likely way this entire ADR's guarantees could be silently
    violated in implementation; Phase 3 review should treat any code path that
    writes more than one of the six states from a single mutation with
    suspicion by default.
13. **Cancellation-charge policy must be reference-table/configuration-backed,
    never hard-coded** (§22) — matching risk 4 above's convention; a
    hard-coded percentage table would also violate ADR-0018 §7.1's "never a
    runtime discriminator" spirit applied to policy rules generally.
14. **No RLS/tenant-isolation risk identified** — cancellation, refund, and
    account-credit rows are ordinary tenant/branch-scoped operational data,
    using the same `ScopedRepository`/RLS pattern as everything else in Part A.
15. **No conflict with Phase 2-core** — identical reasoning to risk 8, Part B
    touches none of `apps/realtime`/`apps/worker`/the outbox schema/the
    idempotency store either.
