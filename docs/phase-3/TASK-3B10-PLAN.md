## 1. What the task is

Task 3b.10 delivers the **first financial reporting** of Phase 3b: read-only, **source-derived**
report endpoints — **trial balance, receivables (AR), customer advances, sales, tender totals** —
with **no new table** (D3b-18, §C.10). The Phase-3 exit criterion it serves: _"trial balance ties;
Owner figures reconcile to the GL and to source transactions."_

It is a backend task. There is no frontend, no export, no realtime update, and no scheduled job in
any frozen document, so there is none in 3b.10.

### Additional frozen semantics

**A. The Trial Balance includes ALL sealed GL.** It never filters on a known `sourceKind` — it is
the complete company ledger, so a new or unknown `sourceKind` still appears. Trial Balance is
**sourceKind-independent**; `sourceKind` mappings belong only to source-derived reports and
reconciliation.

**B. Report date shapes.** Sales: required `from` + `to`. Tender Totals: required `from` + `to`.
Trial Balance: required `from` + `to`. Receivables and Advances: **CURRENT snapshot only** — no
historical `asOf` input; the response may carry a server-derived `asOf` describing when the
snapshot was read. Historical AR / Advance reconstruction is not pretended.

**C. Company by-branch.** Company R2–R5 reports may later return `byBranch` breakdowns. The Trial
Balance does **not** — it is the company GL only (no `byBranch`).

**D. Trial Balance response.** Per account: `opening` = all sealed posting amounts before `from`;
`period` = from `from` through `to` inclusive; `closing` = through `to` inclusive. Returned as exact
minor-unit strings `openingDebitMinor`, `openingCreditMinor`, `periodDebitMinor`,
`periodCreditMinor`, `closingDebitMinor`, `closingCreditMinor`. A debit-minus-credit signed balance
exists only internally, to place an opening / closing balance on the debit or credit side. The
control totals satisfy Σ opening debits = Σ opening credits, Σ period debits = Σ period credits,
Σ closing debits = Σ closing credits. **No pagination.**

## 6. The Trial Balance

**One read statement** (a single consistent snapshot — `runScoped` offers no isolation-level
control, so a multi-statement report could straddle a concurrent posting). Sources: `journal_entry`,
`journal_line`, `account`, plus the one `company` row. No source document is read.

- explicit `tenantId` + `companyId` predicates on every table touched — never RLS or the branch GUC
  alone; **sealed entries only** (`sealedAt IS NOT NULL`);
- **no `sourceKind` predicate**; no branch / POS-terminal / customer predicate; no `LIMIT` / cursor;
- `postingDate < from` → opening · `from ≤ postingDate ≤ to` → period · `> to` → excluded;
- an account is listed iff it has at least one sealed line posted on or before `to` (an account whose
  lines net to zero is still listed — it has activity);
- order: the company's account code, then the immutable account key, then the id, byte-wise (`C`)
  collation;
- the company row is always returned, so "company not found" (zero rows → plain `404 NOT_FOUND`,
  indistinguishable from a foreign tenant's company) differs from "empty ledger" (one row, no account).

**Algorithm (exact `bigint`).** `openingNet = Σdebit(<from) − Σcredit(<from)`;
`closingNet = openingNet + periodDebit − periodCredit`; a non-negative net lands on the debit side, a
negative net on the credit side as its absolute value. Period debit and credit are returned as they
are.

**Fail closed.** `409 REPORT_COMPANY_NOT_CONFIGURED` (no currency / timezone);
`409 REPORT_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (a sealed entry in a currency other than the company's — a
`journal_line` has no currency, it inherits its entry's; nothing is converted or summed);
`500 REPORT_TRIAL_BALANCE_UNBALANCED` (any of the three control-total pairs differs — no figures are
returned).

**Response (internal).** `{ companyId, currencyCode, currencyExponent, accountingTimezone, from, to,
accounts[], totals }`; each account row carries `accountId`, `accountKey`, `category`,
`displayCode`, `displayName` and the six `…Minor` strings; `totals` carries the six `total…Minor`
strings. No `byBranch`, no cursor.

## 7. Verification (Checkpoint A)

Real PostgreSQL, through the real posting engine, the real `flower_app` RLS role and the real scoped
transaction path (every scenario owns a fresh company):

- **Date matrix** — a posting before `from` is opening only; on `from` is period; between is period;
  on `to` is period; after `to` is excluded from every column; a window before the first posting
  returns no accounts with zero totals and full company metadata; an account with activity only after
  `to` is not listed; an account whose lines net to zero is still listed.
- **Timezone / business-date straddle** — in Dubai (UTC+4) an instant that is still `03-31` in UTC
  posts on `04-01` and the report follows the stored posting date; an instant at 23:59:59 local posts
  on `03-31`; the SAME instant is `04-01` in Dubai and `03-31` in Kuwait (UTC+3) and each company
  reports its own — no UTC or client-local conversion anywhere.
- **Document date ≠ postingDate** — a document dated `05-20` whose entry posted on `06-02` is
  invisible to a `05-20` window and present in a `06-02` window.
- **Complete GL** — a sealed journal with a free-text, never-registered `sourceKind` (and the
  `walk_in_sale` / `SETTLEMENT_BATCH` kinds) is included and the totals stay balanced; no source-kind
  registry changes.
- **Sealed only** — a committed, one-sided, UNSEALED entry (written with triggers off — the
  application path cannot commit one) is invisible and does not unbalance the report.
- **Accounts** — asset debit, liability credit, revenue credit and expense debit balances; an account
  that reverses sign across the period in both directions; multi-line / multi-entry accounts;
  deterministic CoA ordering (account code, key, id); an empty ledger is one clean empty report; the
  full CoA is returned with no cursor, limit or page.
- **Money** — AED (exponent 2) with sums beyond `2^53` kept exact; KWD (exponent 3) minor units
  carried unchanged with the exponent 3 reported.
- **Fail closed** — no currency, no timezone → `409 REPORT_COMPANY_NOT_CONFIGURED`; a sealed entry in
  another currency → `409 REPORT_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (a window that excludes it is unaffected); an
  unbalanced sealed ledger → `500 REPORT_TRIAL_BALANCE_UNBALANCED`; invalid dates never reach the
  database.
- **Isolation** — the valid company is served; a sibling company of the same tenant sees only its own
  ledger; a foreign tenant's company, a missing company and a wrong-tenant request are the same plain
  `404 NOT_FOUND` with the same message; no tenant context fails closed.
- **Read-only** — the report transaction is database-enforced READ ONLY (an `UPDATE` inside it is
  rejected) and a report leaves the ledger, `audit_log` and `outbox` row counts unchanged.

## 8. Query-plan / volume gate (OD-3)

**Question.** Can the EXISTING indexes — `journal_entry(tenantId, companyId, postingDate)` and
`journal_line(journalEntryId)` (+ `journal_line(tenantId, companyId)`) — support a first-release Trial
Balance with no migration?

**Method.** `trial-balance.query-plan.integration.test.ts` generates a disposable, chronologically
posted ledger (3 lines per entry over 1 095 days) in a Testcontainers PostgreSQL, alongside other
companies of the same tenant and of a foreign tenant, `VACUUM (ANALYZE)`s it, and runs
`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` on **the exact statement the repository executes**
(`buildTrialBalanceQuery`) through the real RLS role. Each scenario also checks the report against an
independent oracle (per-account sums by a different SQL path) and asserts the plan is not pathological.
Size is configurable (`TB_VOLUME_ENTRIES`, `TB_NOISE_MULTIPLIER`); the committed default (40 000
entries) is a fast CI guard.

**Measurements** — _Local test-container benchmark; not production capacity._ (Default PostgreSQL
settings, two parallel workers, one run per scenario after a warm-up; times are `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`
execution time.)

| Target company (entries / lines) | Whole table (entries) | Target's share | Windows ending at the last day (7 d · 90 d · 365 d · full history) | Window ending at 50 % of history | Window ending at 1 % of history |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| 100 000 / 300 000                | 500 000               | 20 %           | 205 · 228 · 231 · 209 ms                                           | 151 ms                           | 5 ms                            |
| 400 000 / 1 200 000              | 2 000 000             | 20 %           | 1 092 · 1 015 · 968 · 1 117 ms                                     | 1 066 ms                         | 33 ms                           |
| 1 000 000 / 3 000 000            | 2 000 000             | 50 %           | 1 564 · 1 451 · 1 684 · 1 349 ms                                   | 1 156 ms                         | 69 ms                           |

(The CI-volume run — 40 000 entries, 67 % of a 60 000-entry table — is 52–87 ms for the late windows and 2 ms for the 1 % window.) A single-day window costs the same as a 365-day one (212 / 995 / 1 870 ms at the three sizes).

**Plan shapes.**

- _Early close_ (`to` near the start of history): a Bitmap Heap Scan on `journal_entry` through
  **`journal_entry_tenantId_companyId_postingDate_idx`**, then a per-entry Index Scan on
  **`journal_line_journalEntryId_idx`** (nested loop; 402 → 10 046 probes of 3 rows each) — the ideal
  shape, 5–69 ms.
- _Any later `to`_: a (parallel) **Hash Join** into a hash aggregate by account, then a Sort. When the
  company is a small share of the table the planner reads `journal_entry` through the **postingDate
  index** (Bitmap Heap Scan) and `journal_line` through **`journal_line_tenantId_companyId_idx`**;
  when the company is about half of the table it chooses sequential scans, which is the cheaper plan
  there. `account` is joined last on 5–14 rows (negligible). No Nested Loop probes a sequentially
  scanned `journal_line`; there is one statement and no per-account or per-entry round trip.

**Finding.** The cost is driven by the company's TOTAL ledger up to `to` — an opening balance needs
the whole history by definition — and **not** by the length of `from`..`to`: the 7-day, 90-day,
365-day and full-history windows are within about 25 % of each other at every size. It is linear in the
company's journal lines (≈ 0.25 s per 100 000 entries — about 0.8 s per million lines — at a 20 % table share, ≈ 0.5 s per million lines at 50 %). The only ceiling found is the scoped transaction's 20 s timeout, which at the measured rate is
reached at roughly eight to thirteen million entries in a single company on this test container (an observation,
not a capacity statement).

**Decision.** The existing indexes support a first-release Trial Balance. **No migration 50 and no
index change is required.** A period-range cap would NOT reduce the Trial Balance's cost (it is
independent of the span), so **none is recommended for the Trial Balance**; a cap's value for the
later period reports (Sales, Tender Totals) is measured at their own checkpoints. If a company's ledger
ever approaches the transaction timeout, the D3b-18 escape hatch — a rebuildable, reconcilable rollup,
proposed separately — applies; nothing is built for it now. No SLA is claimed.

An invoice has exactly **one** revenue journal: customer-linked → `invoice_ar`, anonymous → `walk_in_sale`. The other kind existing for the same invoice (or a kind that disagrees with the invoice's customer receivable) is a malformed state and fails the report closed. Unknown source kinds are simply not sales — there is no registry and no catch-all.

`walk_in_sale` is the one kind whose producer **exports** its literal (`WALK_IN_SALE_SOURCE_KIND`, `walk-in-sale-journal.ts`), and the frozen Task 3b.9 Checkpoint B pin allows exactly one production file to define it; the report therefore **imports** that constant instead of repeating the literal, so it can never drift from the posting side. The other three kinds are literals in their producers and are pinned against them by the Checkpoint B structural pins. (The first regression run caught the repeated literal against that frozen pin; it was fixed in the report, the 3b.9 pin was not touched.)

### Period authority — the posting date only

A document is in the period iff its **sealed** journal's `postingDate` is. `invoiceDate`, `issuedAt`, `accountingDate` and `createdAt` never decide inclusion. This is not academic: the `invoice_ar` journal is posted without an explicit accounting date, so an invoice issued at 23:59:59 and posted at 00:00:01 (Dubai) has `invoiceDate = 06-14` and `postingDate = 06-15`; the report follows the journal (a real fixture proves both directions). A credit note's `issuedAt` is the wall clock while its journal date is its accounting date; a cancellation charge's explicit accounting date may be in another month than its creation.

### What the report returns

One statement, one consistent snapshot, in the Checkpoint-A read-only scoped transaction. For a **branch** (`branchReport`) or a **company** (`companyReport`, with one `byBranch` row per branch that has activity — each row is exactly that branch's own report):

- **invoices** — `invoiceCount`, `invoicedSubtotalMinor` (the invoice's own subtotal, after line discounts), `lineDiscountMinor`, `documentDiscountMinor` (kept apart, never combined), `outputTaxMinor`, `invoicedTotalMinor`, `salesNetExTaxMinor` = total − tax — exactly the revenue the invoice's journal credits (the net-of-discount convention of decision `3b.9-ACC`; no gross-plus-contra presentation).
- **creditNotes** — `creditNoteCount`, `creditNoteNetExTaxMinor` (= total − tax), `creditNoteTaxMinor`, `creditNoteTotalMinor`, shown separately and never subtracted from the invoice figures; the excess that becomes a CustomerAdvance is never a sales figure.
- **netSalesAfterCreditNotes** — the one explicit net, in signed exact arithmetic: invoice − credit note for ex-tax, tax and total.
- **cancellationCharges** — its own block (net / tax / total), added to nothing else.
- **currentPaymentStatusBreakdown** — the stored `invoicePaymentStatus` of the included invoices for the **full frozen vocabulary** (UNPAID, PARTIAL, PAID, SETTLED, PARTIALLY_REFUNDED, REFUNDED, CANCELLED, VOID), zero-filled, with count and invoice total; PAID and SETTLED stay distinct; the response carries the note _"Payment status is current at report read time; period membership is based on accounting postingDate."_ The status is the invoice's own stored projection — never derived from payments inside the report.
- **reconciliation** — for the three document sets, the source-derived figure against the GL lines of **exactly those journals**: invoice revenue and output tax, credit-note revenue and output-tax reversal, cancellation-charge revenue and output tax. Each control returns `sourceMinor`, `glMinor`, `differenceMinor`, `reconciled`. An unrelated manual journal that credits `REVENUE.SALES` does not participate.

Refunds are **not** sales (Tender reporting); a Payment and a CustomerAdvance are never revenue; nothing is given a financial-result label.

### Immutable discount source — the authority and its integrity proof (owner correction 1)

**What was wrong.** As first delivered, `lineDiscountMinor` was `Σ order_line.discountAmountMinor` on the strength of the freeze trigger alone. `trg_enforce_order_line_freeze` (`BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE ON "order_line"`, migration `20260920120000_orders_invoice_numbering`) rejects an UPDATE or DELETE of a line of an issued order — but an `INSERT` of an extra line into an issued order is **not** trigger-guarded (no migration adds an INSERT trigger on `order_line`), and "no product path performs one" is not a financial proof.

**Discovery — every persisted candidate for an immutable authority, before any change:**

| Candidate                              | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `invoice` row                          | immutable (UPDATE / DELETE trigger-blocked), but holds only `subtotal` = Σ(gross − line discount), `documentDiscount`, `tax`, `total` — **no line-discount total, no line count**. A crafted line with a zero net amount leaves the subtotal and total unchanged. |
| invoice-line / finalized-line snapshot | **none exists** (no `invoice_line` model).                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `order_line` rows                      | UPDATE / DELETE frozen once issued; **INSERT unguarded**.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| order snapshot fields                  | `commercialSnapshotFingerprint` + `commercialSnapshotFingerprintVersion` — **the authority below**.                                                                                                                                                               |
| journal metadata                       | `description` and `postingFingerprint` (a hash of the journal lines, which are net of discount — decision `3b.9-ACC`): no discount information.                                                                                                                   |
| audit snapshot                         | `order.confirmed` `{orderNumber, status}`, `invoice.issued` `{orderId, invoiceNumber}`: no lines, no discount.                                                                                                                                                    |
| outbox event                           | `orders.sale_completed` `{orderId, invoiceId}`: no lines, no discount.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `credit_note_line`                     | `discountCreditedMinor` is derived at credit time from the same `order_line` rows — not independent, and only for credited lines.                                                                                                                                 |
| `payment_attempt`                      | a copy of the same order fingerprint — no new information.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

No stored aggregate holds the line-discount total, so option A (use a persisted total) is unavailable. Option B applies **without any schema change**.

**The authority.** `order.commercialSnapshotFingerprint` (with its immutable `commercialSnapshotFingerprintVersion`) is a SHA-256 over the canonical commercial snapshot of the order: the **ordered full line array** (per line: product, variant, quantity, selected / base UOM and conversion, unit price and currency, discount mode / bps / amount, tax category / rate / effective-from / source), the document discount, the identifying ids and — for version 2 — the fiscal policy. Issuance (`InvoiceIssuanceRepository.issueFinalInvoice`) recomputes it from the locked line set and refuses to issue unless it equals both the stored value and the caller's (`ORDER_FINGERPRINT_MISMATCH`); the order's commercial-freeze trigger then forbids any later change of the column, and `fn_enforce_order_creation_attributes_immutable` freezes the version. It is a persisted, issuance-verified, database-frozen digest of exactly the line set the invoice was issued from. It is **not a count and not a sum**: a crafted line cannot preserve a SHA-256 over the whole ordered set.

**The contract.** `lineDiscountMinor` is the sum of the stored `discountAmountMinor` of an invoice's order lines **only if that invoice's current line set proves itself**:

1. the statement returns, for every invoice of the period, its order's fingerprint inputs and its CURRENT lines (`ORDER BY linePosition`), in the same single statement (same snapshot);
2. `verifyInvoiceLineSets` feeds them to the ONE shared `computeCommercialSnapshotFingerprintByVersion` (imported — no second hashing implementation) and requires the digest to equal the stored fingerprint; the invoice's branch must equal the order's origin branch; a missing order, an empty line set, a malformed line or an unknown version is "not proven";
3. the per-branch line discount and invoice count of the PROVEN invoices must equal the statement's own aggregate (a second, independent sum);
4. any failure fails the whole report closed — `REPORT_SALES_SOURCE_INTEGRITY` (500) naming `invoiceLineSetMismatches` or `lineDiscountCrossCheckMismatches`, never an amount, an id or a figure — and **no figure from an unproven line set is ever returned**.

An extra line (any discount, a zero net amount, even all-zero), a deleted line, a changed discount / quantity / price, a reordering, a price-and-discount pair raised by the same amount, or a changed stored fingerprint therefore cannot alter a successful report undetected. The database itself rejects the UPDATE and DELETE variants (the real-document tests assert both: the database refuses in normal mode, and the report fails closed when the triggers are forced off); the INSERT is accepted by the database and caught by the proof. No catalogue, current price, rate or tax recomputation is involved — the verified lines are only the carrier of the stored `discountAmountMinor`.

**Scope and residual risk.** The proof is bounded to the invoices of the requested period and branch (a tampered invoice elsewhere never disturbs the report; in the requested scope it fails the report). Forcing the triggers off (`session_replication_role = replica`) and rewriting the order's fingerprint together with its lines defeats it — exactly as it defeats the immutable invoice itself; that is outside the threat model of any row-level backstop. **No schema gap was found; no migration is created.** The cost (the statement now ships the order facts and lines of the period's invoices, and Node recomputes one digest per invoice) is covered by the 90-day measurement below.

### Integrity — fail closed, never repaired

The report fails closed (non-disclosing: a check name, never an identifier) when: a journal's `sourceId` names no document of that company, or is not even a uuid (`orphanJournals`); a journal line's branch differs from its document's (`branchMismatchLines`); a document or its journal is in a currency other than the company's (`REPORT_CURRENCY_MISMATCH`, 409); an invoice has both revenue kinds (`duplicateRevenueJournals`); the revenue kind disagrees with the invoice's customer receivable (`revenueKindMismatches`); a credit note's invoice has no sealed revenue journal (`creditNotesWithoutRevenueJournal`); or any reconciliation control differs (`REPORT_SALES_GL_MISMATCH`, 500). An unsealed journal is never authoritative.

**Stated limitation.** A journal-anchored period report cannot see an invoice that has **no** journal at all (finding it would need a scan of every invoice of the company, which would end the report's period-boundedness). Every frozen issuance path posts the journal in the issuing transaction (the public atomic sale, and the customer path through `invoice_ar`); only an internal, route-less primitive could issue a journal-less anonymous invoice. This is recorded as a risk for the final regression, not hidden.

### Owner closure — the density guard: Sales Financial Report v1 limits (90 days AND 25 000 documents)

**The v1 limits (frozen).** One Sales Financial Report invocation is bounded by BOTH:

- at most **90 calendar days, both bounds inclusive** — `REPORT_RANGE_TOO_LARGE` (400), checked before any database work;
- at most **25 000 financial documents** — one authoritative constant, `SALES_REPORT_MAX_DOCUMENTS = 25_000`, and one stable error, `REPORT_RESULT_TOO_LARGE` (**422**).

A **financial document** is an Invoice, a CreditNote or a CancellationCharge (counted through its sealed journal of the period). A Payment, a Refund, a CustomerAdvance, a PaymentAttempt and a SettlementApplication are **not** documents of this report and are never counted. The **company route** counts the whole company (25 000 across all its branches); the **branch route** counts only the requested branch (25 000 in that branch), and a sibling branch never affects it. The **Trial Balance remains uncapped** and the **Tender Totals cap remains undecided** until Checkpoint C measures it.

**Why a second limit — the blocked 100 000-invoice evidence.** The calendar cap bounds elapsed time, not volume. A dense measurement put **100 000 invoices of one company inside one 90-day window** (4 branches, 200 000 order lines as evidence, valid commercial fingerprints, sealed journals): the fixture was produced in bounded batches, the first full-service call took **23.1 s** and the first measured statement **16.8 s** — 84 % of the ≈ 20 s scoped-transaction timeout — plus 0.7 s of parse and 2.3 s of fingerprint proof for a 115 MB payload. The system then stopped that run for low memory and it was **not repeated**; those figures are recorded here as BLOCKED evidence that density, not the calendar, drives the cost. They are not a failure of the report and are not rewritten. _Local test-container benchmark; not production capacity._ (The earlier 400 000-invoice re-measurement that was also stopped for memory is likewise not presented as a failure of the report.)

**The design — one statement, one snapshot, a short-circuiting first stage.**

- The statement gains a first stage `cand`: the sealed journals of the four document kinds joined to their documents by primary key, for a branch report only those of the requested branch, with **`LIMIT 25001`** — so at most 25 001 candidates are ever inspected. `gate` counts them; the count comes back in the same JSON document as `candidateDocuments`.
- Every HEAVY stage — the order / order_line evidence JSON and fingerprint payload (`ils`), the GL aggregate behind the source ↔ GL reconciliation (`gl`), the integrity branch-mismatch scan (`ic`) and the candidates read by every other stage (`docs`) — carries the same comparison `gate.n <= 25000`, which the planner turns into a One-Time Filter **above** the stage's joins: above the limit none of them executes.
- The repository rejects from `candidateDocuments` **before** the company authority, the fingerprint proof or any integrity check. There is no second SQL statement and no COUNT query; an accepted report runs the unchanged fingerprint proof, exact Money, the postingDate authority and the source ↔ GL reconciliation; the scoped-transaction timeout is not raised; no migration.

## 12. Checkpoint C — Tender Totals

Built and verified, **uncommitted**. Internal repository / service only: **no controller, no route, no permission, no migration**, not registered in any Nest module. Checkpoints A and B are frozen — a pin proves their thirteen production files (seven Trial Balance / foundation, six Sales) are byte-unchanged, and the Sales 90-day / 25 000-document rules are not applied to, nor imported by, Tender Totals.

### What Tender Totals is

For one branch, or one company with a `byBranch` breakdown, over a civil period `[from, to]`: **recorded receipts per tender method**, **actual refunds per method** (separately), the **explicit net tender movement** (`netMovementMinor = receiptTotalMinor − refundTotalMinor`, signed, in total and per method) and **source ↔ GL reconciliation controls** for exactly those journals. It owns **no** AR, CustomerAdvance or unapplied-receipt _balance_, no settlement-batch report, no provider-attempt status, no sales or CreditNote revenue and no X / Z shift. The net carries its own label — "Tender movement only … not revenue, not net sales, not a financial result of any kind and not settled money." — and no field anywhere is named revenue, profit, sales, balance or settlement.

### Semantics

- **Receipt = a `Payment` row, once.** An unapplied receipt is a receipt; the PaymentAllocation of a partly applied Payment is not a second one; a CustomerAdvance application, a CreditNote advance, a credit sale and a settlement are never receipts; `CREDIT` is never a method. A **Multi Payment** is one sale and N Payment rows, all N counted once (the term "Mixed Payment" is not used).
- **Method set** = exactly the five frozen `TenderMethod` values, one deterministic zero-filled row per method (`method`, `receiptCount`, `receiptTotalMinor`) — methods are never merged because they share a GL account.
- **Refund = a `Refund` row** (the immutable money-out fact), per method, with its own count and total; a CreditNote is not a refund, a refund does not reduce a receipt or any sales figure.
- **Period authority** = the SEALED journal's `postingDate` only (customer receipt journal; the walk-in journal for an anonymous tender; the refund journal). `Payment.createdAt`, `Refund.createdAt` / `accountingDate`, `invoiceDate` and every settlement date never decide inclusion. Dates are the Checkpoint A civil `YYYY-MM-DD` contract; **no calendar cap and no Sales document limit** applies — the one bound is the movement-density guard (see below).
- **GL controls** (eight, at the narrowest truthful grouping — methods that post to one account share ONE control): receipts → debit of cash / bank / clearing over the customer-receipt and walk-in journals; unapplied receipts → credit over the customer-receipt journals only; refunds → credit of cash / bank / clearing over the refund journals; customer advances → debit over the refund journals. Each control is `{ sourceMinor, glMinor, differenceMinor, reconciled }`.
- **Fail closed, never repaired, non-disclosing** (a check name, never an identifier): `REPORT_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (409) first; then `REPORT_TENDER_SOURCE_INTEGRITY` (500) for any of — an orphan receipt / walk-in / refund journal (including a non-uuid `sourceId` and a document of another company or tenant), a journal line on another branch than its document, a receipt-journal shape that is not exactly `Dr <the Payment's tender account> = amount / Cr unapplied = amount`, a refund-journal shape that is not exactly `Dr advances / Cr <the frozen account>`, a refund whose method has no frozen account, a walk-in journal whose Payments do not equal its debit lines **per account** (a swap that nets to zero in a whole-account control is caught), a walk-in journal on a customer-linked invoice, an anonymous Payment allocated to more than one invoice, a Payment on another branch than its invoice, an anonymous Payment that also carries a receipt journal, an allocated Payment with no receipt journal; then `REPORT_TENDER_GL_MISMATCH` (500) for any control difference. Only SEALED journals are authoritative. A second journal for one source is impossible (the unique `(tenant, company, sourceKind, sourceId)` index — a test proves the database refuses it).
- **Scope**: branch (explicit tenant / company / branch predicates) and company with `byBranch` (the company figures are the exact sum of the branch rows; a branch with no movement is a zero-filled report and is absent from `byBranch`); never a tenant-wide aggregation, never a POS dimension. A defect in another branch does not fail, or appear in, a healthy branch report.
- **ONE statement, ONE database-enforced read-only snapshot**; no write, no audit, no outbox, no realtime, no idempotency, no second query, no N+1. Money leaves the database as TEXT and is parsed strictly to `bigint`; no float, no FX, no rounding.

### Density closure — Tender Totals v1: at most 100 000 logical tender movements per company window

**The rule.** `TENDER_REPORT_MAX_MOVEMENTS = 100 000` — the one authoritative constant (`tender-totals-report.sql.ts`; no other production file repeats the literal). **A movement is exactly one successfully recorded `Payment` or one actual final `Refund`.** The N Payments of a Multi Payment are N movements although ONE `walk_in_sale` journal books them (the gate adds, per candidate sale, its distinct Payments beyond the first); a PaymentAllocation, PaymentAttempt, RefundAttempt (a PENDING or FAILED one posts nothing), CustomerAdvance, CreditNote, SettlementApplication or an invoice / sale row is never a movement. Every candidate journal is worth at least one movement, so more candidate journals than the limit is always over it.

**Why this restriction exists.** The cost of a Tender report is linear in the movements of the window, not in its calendar span (§ above: one dense day costs as much as a 90-day window of the same movement count), and a company that holds ≈ 350 000 tender journals in one request needs ≈ 16 s against the ≈ 20 s scoped-transaction timeout. Without a rule the request would only fail closed with the transaction timeout (never a wrong figure, but a late and opaque failure); the rule makes the failure early, explicit and cheap, and states the instruction that works ("narrow the date range").

**The company window, for the branch route too.** `journal_entry` has no branch column, so a branch report still reads the company's tender journals of the period; the limit bounds that work and is therefore judged on the **company's** window on both routes. A small branch of a dense company is rejected with the same answer as the company (**sparse-company limitation**, stated, not hidden) and is accepted again as soon as the company's window is within the limit.

**The over-limit answer.** `REPORT_RESULT_TOO_LARGE`, HTTP 422, details `maxMovements: 100000` and `action: narrow_date_range` — nothing else. It never reveals the actual count, a branch, a sibling, an id or a financial figure; both routes answer in identical words. Order of checks in the repository: company 404 → branch 404 (a branch of another company is a plain 404, before density) → **density rejection** → period authority → integrity checks. **The over-limit rejection therefore precedes the full integrity analysis**: an over-limit window is rejected without any integrity analysis, and a malformed state inside it is not examined (and is reported once the window is narrowed).

**The design — the same statement, no COUNT-then-report.** The guard is the first stage of the one read statement (one snapshot): `cand` reads at most limit + 1 sealed journals of the three movement kinds in the company window (stopping there — the scan is bounded by the limit, not by the window); `gate` counts them and adds the extra Payments of the candidate walk-in sales in one set-based read (`payment_allocation.invoiceId = ANY (ARRAY(<the candidates' walk-in invoices>))`, grouped per invoice); `candidateMovements` is returned in the same JSON; and the seven heavy stages (`sel`, `cr`, `wj`, `wp`, `rf`, `al`, `jl2`) each carry the gate predicate — a One-Time Filter above their joins — so above the limit none of them executes under any join method (proved by EXPLAIN, not by reading the SQL).

**A defect the performance gate caught, and its fix (recorded, not hidden).** The first gate weighted each walk-in journal with a correlated `COUNT(DISTINCT …)` sub-select. It was exactly right, but PostgreSQL costs a correlated sub-select once per candidate row: the statement's estimated cost became 834 305 (the gate alone 664 588), above `jit_inline_above_cost` / `jit_optimize_above_cost` (500 000), so every execution JIT-compiled all ≈ 420 functions with inlining and optimisation — **≈ 8–10 s of pure compilation, identical for an accepted and a rejected report**. Measured at exactly 100 000 movements: accepted worst **15.6 s** (the 12 s local gate failed — stop condition), rejected 10.1–11.2 s (not cheaper), and on the same data 16.1 s with JIT on against 6.5 s with it off (rejected 10.4 s against 1.0 s; expression-only JIT 8.0 s / 1.8 s). The limit was not touched and no second statement, setting, migration or Checkpoint A change was introduced: the gate's per-journal sub-select was replaced by the one set-based allocation read above (the same exact figure), which brings the plan below the inlining / optimisation threshold (the measured plans now show `Inlining: false`, `Optimization: false`, ≈ 0.6 s of expression JIT). A CI-size EXPLAIN test and the structural pins now pin the shape.

### Stated boundaries (residuals, not hidden)

1. **A document with no journal at all is not provable by a journal-anchored period report** — a Payment or a Refund that no frozen flow booked (finding it would scan every Payment of the company and end the period-boundedness). Every frozen path posts the journal in the document's own transaction; only an internal, route-less primitive or a raw seed can leave one (the integration suite shows the raw-seeded provider Payment of the provider-refund fixture is never counted and never guessed). The two detectors that exist cover what a period _can_ see: an allocation journal in the period whose Payment has no receipt journal at all.
2. **An orphan journal is unattributable to a branch**: it fails the company report closed; a branch report (which cannot own it) does not.
3. **A Payment booked by both streams in different periods** is counted once in each report and fails closed only where one report holds both journals.
4. **A branch report costs like the company's window** (above), and is **judged on it**: a small branch of a company whose window holds more than 100 000 logical movements is rejected with the company's own answer (the density closure, above).
5. **`REPORT_TENDER_GL_MISMATCH` is defence in depth**: the per-journal shape and per-account checks run first, so a database-reachable corruption surfaces as a named integrity check; the pure controls are proven by unit tests and mutation.
6. A provider-finalised refund has no public route (the provider path is an internal primitive until a provider is selected); it is exercised through that primitive — the SUCCEEDED attempts create the Refund and the clearing credit, the PENDING and FAILED ones create nothing.

## 13. Checkpoint D — Receivables current state

Built and verified, **uncommitted**. Internal repository / service only: **no controller, no route, no permission, no migration**, not registered in any Nest module. Checkpoints A, B and C are frozen — a pin proves their seventeen production files (seven Trial Balance / foundation, six Sales, four Tender) are byte-unchanged, and none of the Sales, Tender or Trial Balance rules is applied to, or imported by, the Receivables report. **No Advances implementation exists** (no Advance balance, unapplied-receipt or Advance-creation figure anywhere in the Receivables code).

### What the Receivables report is

For one branch, or one company with a `byBranch` breakdown, optionally narrowed to one customer: the **current receivable position** of the frozen customer receivables — `original`, `paidByPayment` (PaymentAllocation and the receivable payment applications), `paidByAdvance` (CustomerAdvanceApplication), `credited` (the CreditNote **AR reduction only**) and `outstanding` — in total, per **source type** (`INVOICE`, `OPENING`, `CANCELLATION_CHARGE`, always all three, zero-filled), per branch (`byBranch`) and per customer (cursor-paginated aggregate rows), plus the **source ↔ GL Accounts-Receivable control**. It owns no sales, receipt, revenue, settlement, Advance or aging figure; its note says so ("… It is not sales, not receipts, not revenue and not settled money, and it is not a historical figure.") and no field is named sales, receipts, revenue, profit, settled, aging or overdue.

### Owner rulings implemented (Checkpoint D instruction, 2026-10-06)

- **Current snapshot only.** The report has **no date input of any kind** — no `from`, `to`, `asOf`, `date` or period — and no historical reconstruction. `asOf` is **derived by the database in the same statement** (`statement_timestamp()`, ISO-8601 UTC with milliseconds, validated on the way out); the application clock never decides it and a client cannot supply it.
- **No aging.** No due-date contract was ever frozen; no `ageDays`, bucket, overdue or due-date vocabulary exists in the code (pinned).
- **Zero and fully-satisfied receivables stay.** Every receivable is in every total and in the customer rows (there is no open-only filter), so `original − satisfaction = outstanding` stays auditable. (The existing customer-account read model lists open items with `ageDays` for its own screen; this report deliberately does not.)
- **No customer PII.** The customer is reached through `customer_company_account.customerId` only; the customer table, name, phone, e-mail and address are never read, and the serialized report is proven to hold only financial identifiers.

### The frozen contracts — discovered in the producing code, not assumed

| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `original`      | `invoice.totalAmountMinor`                      | `openingAmountMinor`                                    | `cancellation_charge.totalAmountMinor`                  |

`outstanding = original − paidByPayment − paidByAdvance − credited` is **exactly the frozen `computeReceivableBalance`** (`receivables/receivable-balance.ts`): the pure module **calls** the helper once per (source type) cell whose components the one statement summed and never writes the formula a second time (a pin proves it). The helper is linear in its components, so the balance of a sum equals the sum of the balances, and its validation (a closed source-type set, no negative component, no CreditNote reduction on a non-INVOICE receivable) applies to every cell. **A CreditNote's excess that became a CustomerAdvance is never subtracted again and is never receivable** — only `arReductionMinor` is read; the CreditNote total, a CustomerAdvance, a Payment receipt, an unapplied remainder, a Refund and a settlement are not read at all (the statement's table set is pinned).

### The GL Accounts-Receivable control — only the authoritative AR-changing journals

Each producer was read, not remembered. The control never reads the whole `ASSET.ACCOUNTS_RECEIVABLE` account: it selects journals by exactly these eight source kinds, and a manual or unrelated journal that happens to carry an AR line is **not** part of the control (a test posts one and proves it is ignored). The set of AR-touching producers is a **closed set** pinned by a scan of the code base — a new producer of an AR line fails a pin until its AR effect is reviewed.

| Journal `sourceKind`                                                                 | `sourceId`               | AR side | Producer                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `invoice_ar`                                                                         | the Invoice id           | Dr      | `customer-invoice-ar.repository.ts`                          |
| `opening_receivable`                                                                 | the receivable id        | Dr      | `opening-balance.repository.ts`                              |
| `cancellation_charge`                                                                | the charge id            | Dr      | `cancellation-charge.repository.ts`                          |
| `payment_allocation`                                                                 | the PaymentAllocation id | Cr      | `customer-receipt-effects.repository.ts`                     |
| `opening_receivable_payment_application` / `cancellation_charge_payment_application` | the application id       | Cr      | `customer-receipt-effects.repository.ts`                     |
| `customer_advance_application`                                                       | the application id       | Cr      | `customer-advance-application.repository.ts`                 |
| `credit_note`                                                                        | the CreditNote id        | Cr      | `credit-note.repository.ts` (the AR-reduction line **only**) |

Never part of the control: the walk-in sale, a customer receipt (Dr tender / Cr unapplied), an advance conversion, an opening advance, a Refund, a settlement. **One reconciliation, one join.** Every source fact (each receivable and each application) is matched against the AR lines of its ONE sealed journal; unfiltered, a single `FULL OUTER JOIN` on `(sourceKind, sourceId)` classifies every row as matched, fact-only (a **missing journal**) or line-only (an **orphan journal**) in one pass; a branch or customer report is fact-driven (the facts joined to their own journal and AR line through the unique source index). The control is `{ sourceOutstandingMinor, glAccountsReceivableMinor, differenceMinor, reconciled }` with the GL sign debit − credit, built per branch and for the company (the company figure is every branch's journals plus, only on the unfiltered report, an unattributable orphan journal). **A filtered report scopes the totals, the rows and the GL control in the same snapshot** — the customer filter reaches the control, never only the rows.

### Scope, rows and pagination

- **Scope**: the receivable's **own** `branchId` (never the Payment, the POS terminal or the invoice's branch); a branch report uses explicit tenant / company / branch predicates; the company report carries `byBranch` whose rows are the exact figures of each branch report (the company figures are the exact sum of the branch rows; a branch with no receivable is a zero-filled, reconciled report and is absent from `byBranch`); never a tenant-wide aggregation. A branch of another company, a company or customer of another tenant, and a customer with no account at the company are a plain 404; a defect in another branch does not fail, or appear in, a healthy branch report.
- **Per-customer rows**: one aggregate row per customer (`customerId` + the same figures, no PII), keyset-ordered by `customerId` ascending, **default 50, maximum 200** (clamped) — the shared read-model convention (module-private in `customer-account-read.repository.ts`, pinned equal rather than imported), `limit + 1` lookahead, `INVALID_LIMIT` / `INVALID_CURSOR` (400). `nextCursor` is the last **emitted** `customerId` when more customers follow and `null` on the last page (no trailing empty request). The summary, `byBranch` and the GL control are the **whole scope**, never the page.
- **ONE statement, ONE database-enforced read-only snapshot**; no write, no audit, no outbox, no realtime, no idempotency, no second query, no N+1. Money leaves the database as TEXT and is parsed strictly to `bigint`; no float, no FX, no rounding. A test commits concurrent customer receipts (allocation + journal + application in one transaction) while the report is read repeatedly: every report is one coherent state, never a 500, and the last equals the oracle.

### Currency authority, fail closed, never repaired

The report currency is the company's accounting currency (`company.defaultCurrency` + its exponent, Checkpoint A's `resolveCompanyReportAuthority`); AED (2 decimals) and KWD (3 decimals) are both proven. `REPORT_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (409) comes first when a receivable, an application or a journal is in another currency. Then `REPORT_RECEIVABLES_SOURCE_INTEGRITY` (500, a check name only — never an identifier or a figure) for any of: `unknownSourceTypes` (a closed set; the database CHECK also refuses one), `unresolvedPrincipals`, `overCoveredReceivables` (more satisfied than owed — an allocation beyond the invoice, a duplicate allocation, an Advance over-application, a CreditNote AR reduction beyond what remained), `sourceBranchMismatches`, `applicationBranchMismatches`, `paymentApplicationsOnInvoiceReceivables`, `missingJournals`, `journalShapeMismatches` (the AR line on the wrong side or for the wrong amount, or an **extra** AR line in one journal), `journalBranchMismatches`, `orphanJournals`, `creditNotesWithoutReceivable`. Only SEALED journals are authoritative. A second authoritative journal for one source is impossible (the unique `(tenant, company, sourceKind, sourceId)` index; a test proves it). Finally a source-versus-GL difference is `REPORT_RECEIVABLES_GL_MISMATCH` (500) — a figure is **never returned beside a failed control**. A company without an accounting currency / timezone is `REPORT_COMPANY_NOT_CONFIGURED`.

### Stated boundaries (residuals, not hidden)

1. **An unattributable orphan journal fails only the unfiltered company report.** An authoritative-kind AR journal with no source fact has no branch or customer; a branch or customer report is fact-driven and cannot own it, so it neither fails nor reveals it (a test pins both).
2. **The GL control reads only the eight authoritative kinds.** A manual or unrelated journal posted to `ASSET.ACCOUNTS_RECEIVABLE` is deliberately not part of the control (the owner's rule: only authoritative AR-changing journals); it is neither compared nor reported.
3. **`REPORT_RECEIVABLES_GL_MISMATCH` (500) is defence in depth.** The per-fact journal checks (missing, shape, branch, currency, orphan) run first, so a database-reachable corruption always surfaces as a named integrity check; a pure source-versus-GL difference that none of those catches is not independently constructible and is proven by the unit tests and mutation.
4. **A duplicate authoritative journal for one source is impossible** (the unique source index); the duplicate AR _effect_ inside one journal is caught by the shape control.
5. **The pagination constants are pinned equal to the customer-account read model, not imported** (they are module-private there and the frozen file is not edited).
6. **The report lists every receivable; the customer-account screen lists open ones with `ageDays`.** The two are intentionally different products; this one never ages or filters.
7. **Local measurement only.** The 100 000 figures, the ≈ 200 000 projection and the memory observations come from one local test container; the host had < 1 GB free memory at the end of the 100 000 run.
8. **A scope above 100 000 receivables is rejected, not paged (RD-1).** A company that large can only be reported one branch or one customer at a time; the guard counts receivables, not their applications or journals, so a scope within the limit that carries unusually many applications is bounded only by the measured ratios of the delivery ledger (≈ 1.05 allocations, 0.48 receivable payment applications and 1.1 advance applications per receivable).

### Density closure — Receivables v1: at most 100 000 receivables per evaluated scope

**The rule (owner ruling RD-1, APPROVED and IMPLEMENTED 2026-10-06).** `RECEIVABLES_REPORT_MAX_RECEIVABLES = 100 000` — the one authoritative constant (`receivables-report.sql.ts`; no other production file repeats the literal). It is the maximum number of **CustomerReceivable records in the actual evaluated report scope**: the **company** report counts the company's receivables; the **branch** report counts the requested branch's receivables alone (**sibling branches never contribute**); the **customer-filtered** report counts the customer's receivables after the company / branch scope. There is **no calendar cap, no historical `asOf` and no aging** — Receivables v1 stays a current snapshot. All of the owner's cases are proven on real documents: a company above 100 000 is rejected as a company; Branch A with 5 000 and Branch B with 96 000 are each accepted and the company only when the total exceeds 100 000; a customer with 20 receivables inside a company above 100 000 is accepted.

**Why this restriction exists.** The cost of a Receivables report is linear in the receivables of its scope (the volume table above), not in the size of the database: the company report needs 8.0 s p50 / 9.8 s worst at exactly 100 000 receivables — 49 % of the ≈ 20 s scoped-transaction timeout — and grows 2.2–2.4× per doubling above 50 000, so one company scope would reach the timeout at ≈ 200 000 (a projection). Without a rule such a request would only fail with the transaction timeout — never a wrong figure, but a late and opaque failure; the rule makes the failure early, explicit and cheap and states the instruction that works ("narrow the scope to one branch or one customer"). The limit is the largest scope that was **measured**, not an extrapolation, and it is a **local operational safety bound, not a production SLA**.

**The over-limit answer.** `REPORT_RESULT_TOO_LARGE`, HTTP 422 (the code Sales and Tender already use), details `maxReceivables: 100000` and `action: narrow_scope` — nothing else. It never reveals the actual count, a sibling branch, another customer, an id or a financial figure, and every route answers in identical words. Order of checks in the repository: company 404 → branch 404 → customer 404 → **density rejection** → authority (currency / timezone) → integrity analysis. **The over-limit rejection therefore precedes the integrity analysis**: a malformed scope above the limit is rejected for its size and its defect is reported once the scope is within the limit.

**The design — the same statement, no COUNT-then-report.** The gate is the first stage of the one read statement (one snapshot): `gate` reads at most limit + 1 CustomerReceivable rows of the evaluated scope — an early-stopped scan applying exactly the predicates `rcv` applies (tenant, company, the requested branch `$3`, the customer filter `$4` through `customer_company_account`) — and counts them; `candidateReceivables` is returned in the same JSON; and the heavy stages each carry the gate predicate, a One-Time Filter above their joins: the receivables (`rcv`), the four application sources (`app`: PaymentAllocation, receivable payment application, CustomerAdvanceApplication, CreditNote AR reduction), the journal headers and AR lines (`jh`, `jall`), both reconciliation legs (`jx`) and the two integrity counters that read a base table (`ic`) — eleven gate predicates in all. ## 14. Checkpoint E — Customer Advances + Unapplied Receipts current state

Built and verified, **uncommitted**. Internal repository / service only: **no controller, no route, no permission, no migration**, not registered in any Nest module. **Checkpoint letters:** this checkpoint sits ahead of the proposal's original "E — Public HTTP wiring"; the public HTTP wiring is now **F** and the final hard gates **G** (the §2 OD-1 sentence "before public wiring (Checkpoint E)" refers to that public wiring and is left as the owner wrote it). Checkpoints A, B, C and D are frozen — a pin proves their twenty-one production files (seven Trial Balance / foundation, six Sales, four Tender, four Receivables) are byte-unchanged, and none of their rules (the Sales 90-day AND 25 000-document cap, the Tender 100 000-movement cap, the Receivables 100 000-receivable cap) is applied to, or imported by, this report.

### What the report is

For one branch, or one company with a `byBranch` breakdown, optionally narrowed to one customer: the **current position of two financially separate liabilities** — **Customer Advances** and **Unapplied Receipts** — each with its **own** source figures, its **own** source-to-GL control and its own failure, **never netted, never summed, never compared with one another**. Per scope it returns the two blocks, per branch (`byBranch`) the same two blocks, and per customer cursor-paginated aggregate rows (both liabilities, zero-filled where a customer holds only one). It owns no sales, receipt-as-revenue, revenue, profit or settled figure; its note says so ("… two separate liabilities, each reconciled to its own general-ledger account and never netted. It is not sales, not receipts, not revenue and not settled money, and it is not a historical figure.") and the report carries no combined `total`.

### Owner rulings implemented (Checkpoint E instruction, 2026-10-06)

- **Current snapshot only.** **No date input of any kind** — no `from`, `to`, `asOf`, `date` or period — and no historical reconstruction. `asOf` is **derived by the database in the same statement** (`statement_timestamp()`, ISO-8601 UTC with milliseconds, validated on the way out); the application clock never decides it and a client cannot supply it.
- **No aging, no customer PII.** No `ageDays`, bucket, overdue or due-date vocabulary exists in the code (pinned). The customer is reached through `customer_company_account.customerId` only; the customer table, name, phone, e-mail and address are never read, and the serialized report is proven to hold only financial identifiers.
- **One statement, one snapshot.** One read statement in one database-enforced read-only scoped transaction; no write, no audit, no outbox, no realtime, no idempotency key, no second query, no N+1. Money leaves the database as TEXT and is parsed strictly to `bigint`; no float, no FX, no rounding.
- **Pagination** is the shared read-model convention (default **50**, maximum **200** clamped, `limit + 1` lookahead, `INVALID_LIMIT` / `INVALID_CURSOR` 400, keyset on `customerId` ascending, `nextCursor` = the last **emitted** `customerId` while more follow and `null` on the last page); the constants are module-private in `customer-account-read.repository.ts` and are **pinned equal**, not imported. The summaries, `byBranch` and both GL controls are the **whole scope**, never the page.

### Customer Advances — the frozen contract, discovered in the producing code, not assumed

**Origins (`customer_advance.sourceType`, a database CHECK):** exactly `PAYMENT` (a Payment converted into an Advance), `OPENING` (an opening advance) and `CREDIT_NOTE` (a CreditNote's excess, funded through `credit_note_coverage_release`). The breakdown `bySourceType` is always these three, in this order, zero-filled; an unknown type is an integrity failure, never an "else PAYMENT".

**The balance is exactly the frozen `computeAdvanceBalance`** (`receivables/receivable-balance.ts`): the pure module **calls** the helper once per (source type) cell whose components the one statement summed and never writes the formula a second time (a pin proves it).

| Figure                          | Meaning                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `originalAdvanceMinor`          | Σ `customer_advance.amountMinor` (the principal)                                                                           |
| `appliedMinor`                  | Σ `customer_advance_application` (an Advance applied to a receivable)                                                      |
| `actuallyRefundedMinor`         | Σ `customer_advance_refund_application` (a refund that **actually happened** — local, or a provider refund that SUCCEEDED) |
| **`bookLiabilityMinor`**        | `original − applied − actuallyRefunded` — **the figure the GL control judges**                                             |
| `pendingRefundReservationMinor` | Σ `refund_attempt_entitlement_reservation` of attempts in state **PENDING** only                                           |
| **`availableMinor`**            | `bookLiability − pendingRefundReservation` — what may be applied or refunded **now**                                       |

**A pending reservation reduces `availableMinor` only.** It is not in the book liability and not in any GL fact (it posts no journal while PENDING); a **FAILED** attempt reserves nothing and a **SUCCEEDED** attempt has become an actual refund application — so only PENDING is read, a pin proves the state predicate appears once and no other state is named. A reservation beyond the available balance, an over-applied or over-refunded advance (book < 0) fails the report closed. A CreditNote's excess is an Advance **and only an Advance**: it is reached through its coverage release and summed once per CreditNote; the CreditNote itself, its total and its excess column are never read, and the excess is never an Unapplied-Receipt figure.

### Unapplied Receipts — the frozen contract

The universe is the customer-linked Payments: `CUSTOMER_RECEIPT` through the attempt's own customer account, `INVOICE_COLLECTION` through the invoice's order customer (a walk-in sale has no customer account and is in neither liability). **A receipt is counted once** — one `payment` row, one receipt fact — and a PaymentAllocation, a receivable payment application or a conversion only ever **reduce** its unapplied remainder.

`unappliedReceiptMinor = original receipt − allocated to invoices − applied to receivables (opening / cancellation charge) − converted to an Advance` — **exactly the frozen `computePaymentConsumption`** with both draws on receivables in its first term, which is the database capacity trigger's own three-term formula (`payment_allocation` + `customer_receivable_payment_application` + the Advances sourced from the Payment). The figures: `paymentCount`, `paymentCountWithUnapplied`, `originalReceiptMinor`, `paymentAllocationMinor`, `receivablePaymentApplicationMinor`, `allocatedToReceivablesMinor` (their sum), `convertedToAdvanceMinor`, `unappliedReceiptMinor`. A `CustomerAdvanceApplication` is **not** a receipt consumption (it reduces an Advance, which was funded by the conversion): it is counted once, as an Advance reduction, and no unapplied stage reads it. An over-consumed Payment fails the report closed.

**Payment → Advance conversion moves the liability once.** The relation is exactly `customer_advance.sourcePaymentId`; the conversion posts **one** journal (`customer_advance`: **Dr `LIABILITY.UNAPPLIED_RECEIPTS` / Cr `LIABILITY.CUSTOMER_ADVANCES`**), so the unapplied remainder falls and the advance book liability rises by the same amount, no second receipt is created, and the Tender report's receipts are unchanged (a test proves all three). An `OPENING` or `CREDIT_NOTE` advance is never a conversion and never debits the unapplied account.

### The two GL controls — only the authoritative journals of each account

Each producer was read, not remembered. Neither control reads a whole account: each selects journal lines by source kind **per account**, so a manual or unrelated journal posted to either liability account is **not** part of a control (a test posts one and proves it is ignored). The set of producers of a line on either account is a **closed set** pinned by a scan of the code base — a new producer fails a pin until its liability effect is reviewed.

| Control            | Account                        | Journal `sourceKind` (`sourceId`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer Advances  | `LIABILITY.CUSTOMER_ADVANCES`  | `customer_advance` (the Advance id, Cr) · `opening_advance` (the Advance id, Cr) · `credit_note` (the CreditNote id, Cr — the excess line) · `customer_advance_application` (the application id, Dr) · `refund` (the Refund id, Dr)                      |
| Unapplied Receipts | `LIABILITY.UNAPPLIED_RECEIPTS` | `customer_receipt_payment` (the Payment id, Cr) · `payment_allocation` (the allocation id, Dr) · `opening_receivable_payment_application` / `cancellation_charge_payment_application` (the application id, Dr) · `customer_advance` (the Advance id, Dr) |

`customer_advance` is the **one** kind on both controls — the conversion is the single journal that moves a liability from one account to the other; the liability **increases** are credits and every reduction a debit. Never part of a control: the walk-in sale, an invoice AR journal, a settlement, any manual adjustment. **One reconciliation, one join per control.** Every source fact is matched against the line of its ONE sealed journal on its own account; unfiltered, a single `FULL OUTER JOIN` on `(control, sourceKind, sourceId)` classifies every row as matched, fact-only (a **missing journal**) or line-only (an **orphan journal**) in one pass; a branch or customer report is fact-driven (the facts joined to their own journal and line through the unique source index). Each control is `{ source…, gl…, differenceMinor, reconciled }` with the GL sign **credit − debit** (a liability), built per branch and for the company (the company figure is every branch's journals plus, only on the unfiltered report, an unattributable orphan journal). **A filtered report scopes the totals, the rows and both GL controls in the same snapshot.** **The two controls are judged independently:** an Advance difference of +X and an Unapplied difference of −X can never net to a pass — either failing control fails the report closed (`REPORT_LIABILITIES_GL_MISMATCH`, 500, naming only the control(s)), and a figure is never returned beside a failed control.

### Stated boundaries (residuals, not hidden)
